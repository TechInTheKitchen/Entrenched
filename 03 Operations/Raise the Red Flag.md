# Introductory Operation: Raise the Red Flag

This operation teaches the complete cycle of **Entrenched**: cross known ground, discover a route, mark the map, die, return altered, and use what the unit learned to push farther.

It is written for **three to six sappers** and should last two to four sessions.

## Briefing

Enemy troops hold **Trench 19**, a forward position overlooking the Allied line. At dawn in several days, a larger assault will advance only if the sappers raise a red signal flag over the trench.

The unit must cross no-man's-land, breach Trench 19, silence its defenders, and keep the flag standing through one complete enemy turn. Command knows the trench's approximate location and provides a flag, a rough map, and no promise of support.

The operation succeeds when the red flag remains raised at the end of an enemy turn. It fails permanently only when the entire unit has become Battleborn and is captured or destroyed beyond revival.

## What Command Knows

Mark the following on the players' map before the first run:

- The Allied trench along the southern edge
- The approximate position of Trench 19 near the northern edge
- One ruined road or drainage line pointing toward the enemy
- A broad area believed to contain mines
- A visible landmark such as a shattered chapel, dead tree, or burnt tank

Command does not know the safe routes, patrol schedule, usable shelter, precise wire layout, or the location of the trench's machine gun.

## Recommended Supplies

Command recommends the following before the first outfitting:

- Wire cutters or a Bangalore section for the outer belts
- A mine probe, marking flags, or another way to identify a safe lane
- Field glasses for locating the trench's firing positions
- Smoke grenades or camouflage for crossing exposed ground
- Satchel charges for the wire, machine-gun position, or weak revetment
- A Far-range weapon for sentries and trench defenders
- Field dressings or a Butcher's field roll for recovery near the objective

This list is incomplete intelligence, not a required loadout. Place at least two useful pieces of salvage beyond the Allied line: one among an earlier unit's remains near the Approach and another in a dangerous cache or wreck within the Killing Ground. Anything brought back may be formally issued on a later run. The players should have to choose between carrying the ideal tool now and learning where another can be recovered later.

## Drawing the Map

Use a roll of butcher paper, kraft paper, the blank back of wrapping paper, or paper with a **one-inch square grid**, plus a dark marker and a **ten-minute time limit**. The map should feel like an officer's hurried battlefield sketch, not a finished tactical diagram.

Draw a field **36 squares wide** and **84 squares long** for the introductory operation. A long roll can sit across or beside the table. If the available paper or table is smaller, tape sheets together or divide the field into connected Approach, Killing Ground, and Enemy Works sections while keeping one continuous coordinate system.

During the ten minutes:

1. Draw the Allied trench across the southern edge.
2. Draw Trench 19 across part of the northern edge.
3. Add three large landmarks visible from a distance.
4. Draw two broken routes from south to north: a road, drainage ditch, communications trench, ridge, or crater chain.
5. Add six to ten areas of difficult or exposed ground.
6. Add three to five pieces of artillery shelter, such as burnt tanks or deep ruins.
7. Leave open space. Unmarked ground gives the operation room to grow.

Do not try to detail every square. After the timer ends, secretly add the prepared obstacles, routes, and enemy positions below. Add further findings during play whenever exploration, retaliation, or player curiosity makes them relevant.

## Prepared Features

Place these features secretly before play:

- **The wire:** Two belts guard Trench 19. One is obvious; the other is partly buried by mud.
- **The minefield:** It blocks the most direct route but contains one narrow, discoverable passage.
- **The machine gun:** It covers the obvious breach and one exposed approach.
- **The patrol:** It follows a repeatable route through the middle third of the map.
- **The shelter:** A burnt tank or deep cellar protects against artillery but contains evidence of an earlier unit.
- **The hidden approach:** A drainage cut, crater chain, or collapsed tunnel reaches the enemy wire with less exposure.
- **The reinforcement route:** A communications trench allows new defenders to reach Trench 19 between runs.
- **The signal position:** A visible point where the red flag can be raised.

The unit should be able to discover at least two viable approaches. No single failed check should make the objective impossible.

## Discoveries

Reveal or invent discoveries as the sappers explore. Useful findings include:

- A marked gap in the minefield
- The patrol's route and timing
- A crater deep enough for field revival
- A corpse carrying cutters, explosives, or part of an old map
- A blind angle beneath the machine gun's traverse
- A culvert that passes under one wire belt
- A listening post connected to the enemy trench
- A weak revetment that can be collapsed
- A field telephone wire leading toward the reinforcement trench
- An abandoned ammunition cache that can be detonated

Every run should reveal something worth adding to the players' map, even when the unit gains little ground.

## Enemy Forces

Use ordinary enemy soldiers from [[Enemies and Monsters]]. Begin with enough defenders to make a frontal attack suicidal rather than impossible:

- One patrol of two soldiers, plus one additional soldier for a unit of five or six sappers
- Two sentries near the outer wire
- One machine-gun team in Trench 19
- Two trench defenders, plus one additional defender for every sapper beyond three

These numbers describe positions, not a balanced encounter. The unit should weaken, bypass, isolate, or surprise them across repeated runs.

Roll every enemy attack openly.

## Enemy Reactions

After each run, choose one response the enemy could justify:

- Replace a destroyed patrol on the same route with altered timing
- Add a sentry to a route where sappers were seen
- Place bodies or abandoned equipment as bait
- Repair part of the wire while leaving visible evidence
- Aim the machine gun toward a revealed approach
- Mine a route the unit used repeatedly
- Move defenders from Trench 19 to protect the reinforcement trench

Permanent damage remains permanent. A destroyed machine gun, collapsed tunnel, cut reinforcement trench, or detonated ammunition cache is not restored unless the fiction gives the enemy time and means to restore it.

## Taking Trench 19

Reaching the trench is not enough. The unit must create a breach, overcome or bypass the defenders, and spend an action raising the red flag at the signal position.

The flag must remain standing through the next enemy turn. The enemy may attack its bearer, cut it down, or attempt to reclaim the position. If the flag survives until the following sapper turn, Allied whistles answer from the southern trench and the operation succeeds.

## Trench Interludes

Use brief scenes to show the operation changing the Allied trench:

- After the first run, a map clerk carefully copies the unit's marks while stretchers arrive behind them.
- After the first Battleborn transformation, nearby soldiers stop using the sapper's name.
- When the unit finds the hidden approach, an officer promises that thousands of lives now depend upon it.
- Before the final run, the Butchers have already laid out replacement parts beside empty tables.

## Further Complications

If the unit is progressing too easily, add one complication that follows from play:

- Artillery begins targeting the most-used route.
- A corpse hound starts following the unit's scent.
- Rain floods the hidden approach but exposes another entrance.
- An enemy sapper rebuilt from a player's former limb recognizes them.
- Trench 19 begins recovering and reviving its dead.

Do not add complications simply to erase success. Each should create another choice or reveal another route.

## Debrief

Once the flag rises, Allied troops advance past whatever remains of the unit. Ask each surviving player:

- What part of the map will future soldiers know by their sapper's name?
- Which borrowed memory do they now mistake for their own?
- Do they still recognize the red flag as a sign of victory?

The war continues. This operation does not.
