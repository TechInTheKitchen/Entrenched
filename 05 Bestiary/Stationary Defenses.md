# Stationary Defenses

Stationary defenses are not ordinary enemies waiting to exchange attacks. They are sections of the battlefield built to make a direct approach impossible: pillboxes, buried projectors, sealed gun rooms, observation cupolas, and living fortifications whose operators may never be visible. Use the normal checks, cooperation, attacks, and visibility rules from [[Skill Checks and Combat]] except where a profile below replaces them. [[Specialties and Field Doctrines|Field Doctrines]] can reveal or exploit an opening but never erase a defense's Protection.

Run an emplacement like a **trap with a crew**, not a combatant with another turn. It threatens a known piece of ground, reacts when its trigger is met, and becomes vulnerable only when the sappers learn how it works or force it to look the wrong way.

The unit should solve an emplacement through observation, distraction, concealment, sabotage, terrain, or cooperation. Gunfire may finish the solution, but firing at a sealed wall is not one.

## Reading an Emplacement Profile

Each stationary defense lists:

- **Tell:** What careful sappers can notice before entering its killing ground.
- **Coverage:** The squares, route, structure, or area it can threaten.
- **Trigger:** What causes it to retaliate.
- **Retaliation:** What happens when it fires or activates.
- **Protection:** Why it cannot be defeated head-on.
- **Openings:** Conditions that expose its crew, mechanism, ammunition, or blind approach.
- **Defeat:** What permanently disables or destroys it.

Mark confirmed coverage, triggers, blind ground, and successful sabotage on the shared map. What the unit learns survives the sappers who learned it.

## The Emplacement Cycle

Most stationary defenses follow three steps.

### 1. Read the Killing Ground

Describe at least one Tell before the defense activates. The players may investigate it with Awareness, Stealth, Engineering, or another suitable approach. Success reveals the trigger, the shape of its coverage, or one opening. Failure provokes the emplacement's retaliation if the investigating sapper is exposed to it.

A previously mapped trigger or firing lane remains known on later runs unless the enemy visibly changes it.

### 2. Create an Opening

The unit must make the defense unable to cover everything at once. Common methods include:

- One sapper draws its attention while another moves through a blind area.
- Smoke, darkness, debris, or suppressing fire blocks its sight.
- A false sound, thrown object, animal, or moving corpse triggers it at the wrong time.
- A sapper remains hidden until a firing slit, projector door, vent, or crew member is exposed.
- The unit reaches a tunnel, drainage culvert, roof, rear door, ammunition line, or observation cable.
- Earlier sabotage lowers an operation-level Resistance or removes one of the defense's protections.

A successful distraction holds the emplacement's attention on one declared target or area until the beginning of the distractor's next turn. It cannot retaliate against a different approach during that window unless another profile rule says otherwise. The distractor remains exposed to its retaliation.

### 3. Exploit the Opening

Once an Opening exists, another sapper may act against the exposed part. The action might be a Ranged attack through a firing slit, a Close Quarters attack on the crew, an Engineering check against a feed mechanism, Demolitions against ammunition or supports, or movement through a concealed route.

During combat, leading and helping with this action consume the participants' actions as normal. The emplacement's listed trigger explicitly allows it to retaliate during combat; it does not also take an enemy turn.

## General Rules

### No Head-On Attack

An intact emplacement's outer structure cannot be attacked with ordinary weapons. Its crew and working parts cannot be targeted until an Opening exposes them. A weapon or charge specifically capable of destroying the structure treats it as an operation-level obstacle, normally Resistance 4, and still requires preparation that reduces that Resistance.

For example, a reinforced pillbox begins at Resistance 4 and cannot be breached directly. Cutting its reinforcement trench reduces it to 3. Reaching and detonating its buried ammunition supply reduces it to 2.

### Triggered Retaliation

An emplacement retaliates when its Trigger occurs, even during combat. It does not act merely because an enemy turn begins. Unless a profile says otherwise, it retaliates no more than once during each sapper turn; several sappers moving together may all be threatened by one explicitly multi-target retaliation.

If one attack threatens several targets, make one roll and compare its successes separately with each target's Defense. Apply one lethal hit to every target whose Defense is met.

### Attention and Distraction

A defense capable of aiming has **Attention**. Its Attention rests on the last area where it detected a credible target. A sapper may draw Attention by becoming visible, making a loud attack, deliberately failing concealment, or succeeding on a suitable check. The referee states whether the attempted distraction is credible before dice are rolled.

If the distraction succeeds, another route becomes an Opening. If it fails, the defense retaliates against the distractor and keeps its previous coverage.

Mindless, automated, or living defenses may be distracted by the stimulus described in their profile rather than by ordinary soldiers.

### Hidden Sappers and the Perfect Shot

A sapper hidden outside the defense's current Attention may wait without making repeated checks. When an Opening appears, they may use their next action to attack the exposed target. The profile gives that target's Defense. Success disables the listed part or kills the exposed operator; it does not damage the surrounding fortification.

If the shot fails during combat, the emplacement does not gain an extra retaliation unless the shot itself meets its Trigger, such as muzzle flash revealing the sapper inside its coverage.

### Bypassing and Returning

Bypassing a defense does not destroy it. Mark the safe route and the still-active coverage separately. The emplacement may threaten retreat, recovery, reinforcements, or later runs until disabled.

A disabled defense remains disabled unless the enemy has a surviving route, crew, and enough time to repair it. Repairs should leave a Tell and should not erase the unit's knowledge of the underlying position.

## Conventional Emplacements

### Machine-Gun Pillbox

A low concrete bunker covers a road, wire gap, trench approach, or broad firing lane. Its gun fires from a slit too narrow to assault from the front.

- **Tell:** Cut vegetation, rows of old impacts, ammunition links in the mud, a narrow dark slit, or ground conspicuously free of bodies.
- **Coverage:** One marked lane up to Far range, normally 4–8 squares wide.
- **Trigger:** Visible movement, muzzle flash, or a loud impact within the lane.
- **Retaliation:** The gun makes one 7-dice Far attack. If several sappers cross the lane together, the same roll threatens each of them.
- **Protection:** The concrete shell and recessed slit cannot be attacked head-on. The hidden crew has Defense 2 when exposed.
- **Openings:** Attention drawn to one edge of the lane; smoke across the slit; a drainage route beneath its fire; the gun barrel exposed while traversing; a rear entrance, ammunition feed, or observation cable reached from outside its coverage.
- **Defeat:** Kill the exposed gunner, jam or sabotage the feed at Resistance 2 Engineering, place explosives at the slit or rear door at Resistance 2 Demolitions, or collapse its supports after reducing its operation-level Resistance.

The pillbox is the model for a distraction and perfect-shot puzzle. One sapper can draw bursts against hard shelter while a hidden rifleman waits for the gunner to lean into the slit. Another unit might fill the lane with smoke and crawl to the rear door instead.

### Armored Observation Cupola

A steel dome watches several approaches and directs artillery it does not fire itself.

- **Tell:** A turning lens, field telephone wire, ranging flags, fresh shell patterns, or a dome on terrain too exposed for an ordinary sentry.
- **Coverage:** Every visible area within Far range, divided by solid barriers and terrain.
- **Trigger:** The cupola observes a sapper remain in one area, use explosives, or cross a previously watched route.
- **Retaliation:** Mark the observed square. At the end of the next sapper turn, a shell attacks everyone still within 2 squares with 8 dice. The mark remains visible before impact.
- **Protection:** The armored dome cannot be harmed by ordinary firearms. Its observer has Defense 2 when a vision shutter opens.
- **Openings:** Feed it movement at a false target; sever or follow its telephone wire; approach through dead ground; reflect light into the lens; wait hidden for the shutter to open while it confirms a target.
- **Defeat:** Kill the observer through the open shutter, cut the communication line at Resistance 1 Engineering, enter through its buried access, or destroy the dome's foundation at Resistance 3 Demolitions after reaching it.

### Retractable Trench Cannon

A heavy gun rises from a protected pit, fires once, and sinks below an armored cover to reload.

- **Tell:** Parallel rails, hydraulic fluid, a semicircle of blast-cleared mud, shell racks, or a crew trench leading to an apparently empty pit.
- **Coverage:** One broad Far firing arc shown on the referee's map.
- **Trigger:** A vehicle, formation, fortification, or several sappers enter the arc together.
- **Retaliation:** The cannon makes one 10-dice attack against one target or one occupied square, then retracts. Everyone in a targeted square is threatened by the same roll.
- **Protection:** It cannot be targeted while retracted. The raised gun has Defense 4; its loading crew has Defense 2.
- **Openings:** Bait the gun into firing at false movement; jam its cover while raised; enter the loading trench during its reload; sabotage the rails or hydraulic line.
- **Defeat:** Jam it at Resistance 2 Engineering during the firing window, kill the exposed crew, detonate its shell rack, or place a charge beneath the retracted platform.

## Flame and Gas Emplacements

### Flame Casemate

A buried fuel reservoir feeds a projector behind an armored shutter. It denies trenches, tunnels, and narrow approaches rather than sweeping an entire field.

- **Tell:** Oily condensation, dead ground without intact corpses, heat shimmer, fuel pipes, scorched brick, or the click of a shutter cycling.
- **Coverage:** A Close cone or one trench section extending 6 squares from the projector.
- **Trigger:** Warm movement, vibration on a pressure line, or any sapper crossing the marked threshold.
- **Retaliation:** Everyone in the covered area is attacked once with 7 dice. A sapper killed by the flame cannot be revived unless recoverable material remains. A Battleborn death marks Burn normally; a result that explicitly completely incinerates the remains fills every remaining Burn box.
- **Protection:** The projector sits behind a shutter that opens only to fire. The nozzle has Defense 3 while open.
- **Openings:** Trigger it with a thrown object or moving corpse; cool or mask a sapper's heat; cut the pressure line; flood the trench; approach along the fuel pipe; wait hidden for the shutter.
- **Defeat:** Shoot or jam the open nozzle, close the fuel valve at Resistance 2 Engineering, rupture the reservoir from a safe route, or redirect the projector into its own position.

Rupturing fuel without first learning its route may turn the surrounding ground into a larger fire hazard. Telegraph this clearly.

### Gas Projector Nest

A sealed bunker pumps gas through buried pipes and low vents. Its true weapon is the ground around it.

- **Tell:** Dead insects, tarnished metal, low vapor, masks nailed near safe passages, buried ceramic pipe, or wind flags watched by a sealed crew.
- **Coverage:** One depression, trench network, tunnel, or connected group of marked squares. Wind or ventilation determines where gas moves.
- **Trigger:** A trip line, opened hatch, detected movement, or a remote observer's signal.
- **Retaliation:** Fill the first covered area with gas and move it 2 squares with the wind at the end of each sapper turn. A sapper entering or beginning a turn in it must pass a Resistance 1 Mobility check using a working respirator or mark a Flesh box. This explicitly applies during combat.
- **Protection:** The sealed pumping room cannot be attacked through the cloud. Its external valves and vents can be reached.
- **Openings:** Move upwind; block or reverse a vent; follow a clean-air pipe; create a false trigger while the unit waits outside the spread; enter through a maintenance line; turn the gas toward enemy works.
- **Defeat:** Shut the master valve at Resistance 2 Engineering, destroy the pump after reaching its service room, collapse the feed tunnel, or ignite an identified gas that the referee has clearly established as flammable.

Gas remains on the map until wind, ventilation, or deliberate action clears it. Respirators permit the check; they do not make the area safe.

## Reconstructed and Unnatural Emplacements

### Corpse-Recognition Gun

A pillbox that has no living gunner. Preserved eyes and noses are wired around its slit, and the gun fires at bodies it does not recognize.

- **Tell:** Jars facing the field, a wet clicking behind the slit, bodies arranged by uniform, or a firing lane interrupted around enemy corpses.
- **Coverage:** One Far lane or approach.
- **Trigger:** The organs see or smell an unrecognized living body within coverage.
- **Retaliation:** A 7-dice Far attack against the detected target. Several sappers deliberately moving together can be threatened by one roll.
- **Protection:** Killing a crew cannot stop it. The sensing cluster has Defense 3 and is visible only when its lids unfold to identify a target.
- **Openings:** Wear enemy material; move behind a recognized corpse; overwhelm one sense with smoke, blood, or decay; let one disguised sapper hold its Attention; approach through a route with no line to the organ cluster.
- **Defeat:** Destroy the exposed cluster, replace its scent reservoir, sever its preserved nerves at Resistance 2 Engineering, or feed it donor material that makes the Allied unit register as recognized.

A failed disguise should reveal which sense rejected it before the gun fires. The death must teach the next run something.

### Choir Aperture

Human throats are mortared into a bunker wall. They listen for speech, repeat it in the voices of the dead, and direct a hidden weapon toward anyone who answers.

- **Tell:** Whispered repetitions, names spoken from inside solid masonry, wax-filled enemy ears, or firing lanes aimed at places where echoes carry.
- **Coverage:** A Close network of trenches, rooms, or ruins connected by sound.
- **Trigger:** Speech, shouted orders, gunfire, or a sapper answering an imitated voice.
- **Retaliation:** The Choir fixes the speaker's location. A concealed gun makes a 6-dice attack that ignores smoke but not solid barriers.
- **Protection:** The throats are spread throughout the structure. Destroying one does not silence the Choir.
- **Openings:** Use hand signals; create sound somewhere else; speak through a long pipe; follow which throats answer first; let a borrowed echo recognize and complete the Choir's phrase.
- **Defeat:** Sever the central vocal cord at Resistance 2 Engineering, drown the chambers, destroy the resonating room, or use its repeated commands to direct enemy defenders into the wrong position.

Hearing the Choir clearly may qualify for Humanity loss from perceiving the other side, but do not charge Humanity merely for hearing one unsettling voice.

### Black-Root Ballista

A buried mass of Black Matter grows bone shafts and launches them toward vibration. It has no sight, crew, or allegiance.

- **Tell:** Parallel punctures through stone, bone splinters pointing backward toward one patch of earth, complete stillness among nearby creatures, or roots tightening beneath a footstep.
- **Coverage:** A Far line through connected earth. Deep water, suspended flooring, and broken ground interrupt it.
- **Trigger:** Repeated footsteps, digging, explosions, or a heavy impact touching the same earth.
- **Retaliation:** The root launches a 9-dice Far attack through the strongest vibration. The projectile can cross smoke and darkness but not disconnected ground.
- **Protection:** The buried organism cannot be targeted from the surface. Its firing pore has Defense 3 during the instant it opens.
- **Openings:** Move over debris without touching earth; create a stronger vibration elsewhere; flood or sever the soil; remain still until another creature draws the shot; expose the root through an existing crater.
- **Defeat:** Strike the open pore, sever three marked root junctions at Resistance 1 Engineering each, poison it with a prepared source, or lure it into repeatedly firing until it excavates its own core.

Touching an active root is contact with Black Matter and costs Humanity as normal.

### Ossuary Gate

A passage has been sealed with interlocked bodies that tense when approached. The gate rebuilds itself from nearby remains and opens only for movements its borrowed nerves remember.

- **Tell:** Hands worn smooth at repeated gestures, uniform fragments from many armies, footprints ending at the wall, or faces turning toward familiar voices.
- **Coverage:** One doorway, trench throat, tunnel, or narrow route.
- **Trigger:** Touch, forced entry, an unfamiliar gait, or damage to the outer layer of bodies.
- **Retaliation:** The gate makes a 7-dice Close attack with grasping limbs. A killed sapper is pulled into the structure at the end of the next sapper turn unless freed.
- **Protection:** Ordinary attacks remove flesh that immediately regrows from nearby remains. It has no meaningful Defense while the rebuilding supply remains connected.
- **Openings:** Imitate a donor's remembered movement; present a matching body part; remove nearby remains; follow an enemy through; trigger the grasp elsewhere while a hidden sapper reaches the nerve knot.
- **Defeat:** Sever its supply of bodies, cut the central nerve knot at Resistance 2 Close Quarters or Engineering during an Opening, burn the established regrowth source, or teach it a new recognized gesture through repeated donor contact.

A sapper incorporated into the gate is recoverable until the next run unless the operation establishes that the structure moves or consumes its bodies sooner.

## Building New Stationary Defenses

Start with a battlefield function rather than a weapon name:

- Deny a route
- Watch an area
- Separate the unit
- Prevent body recovery
- Protect a supply or reinforcement line
- Force movement into another hazard

Then give the defense:

1. One clear Tell before activation.
2. One specific Trigger.
3. One dangerous Retaliation.
4. One reason a frontal attack cannot work.
5. At least three Openings using different skills or pieces of terrain.
6. One permanent way to disable it and one way merely to bypass it.
7. One discovery that remains valuable after everyone dies.

Do not hide every solution behind checks. A plan that directly defeats the trigger should work. Roll when execution under danger remains uncertain, not to make the players prove that a solved puzzle is still solved.

## Referee Principles

- **Show the killing field before the weapon.** Bodies, craters, silence, and altered terrain reveal coverage.
- **Let the defense be consistent.** A trigger that changes without cause turns a puzzle into a guess.
- **Attack the approach, not every turn.** The emplacement reacts when the sappers give it something to react to.
- **Reward divided roles.** One sapper distracts, one observes, one moves, and one exploits the opening.
- **Keep several answers alive.** Concealment, sabotage, terrain, equipment, donor memories, and violence should all matter.
- **Make bypass different from victory.** A route around the gun helps now; a silenced gun changes every later run.
- **Let death reveal procedure.** If the emplacement kills a sapper, the unit should learn where it looked, what woke it, or how long it remained open.
