# The Butchers

Butchers are the reconstruction surgeons of the Allied forces. Their title began as trench slang and became an official rank after no cleaner word remained credible.

Their workshops resemble abattoirs built inside chapels. Bodies hang from numbered hooks. Donor limbs rest in brine or twitch beneath damp cloth. Tiled channels carry blood and Black Matter toward separate drains, although both pipes disappear into the same lower wall.

## Appearance

Butchers wear waxed aprons over surgical gowns that were once white. Senior Butchers adopt the gold vestments of the [[Faith of the Final Trench]], marking their claimed authority over the border between life and death. Their tools are kept immaculate even when everything around them is black with old blood.

Many replace their own fingers with narrow jointed instruments. This is presented as professional devotion. Assistants whisper that natural hands eventually begin remembering too many patients.

## The Workshop

A typical workshop contains:

- Intake hooks labeled with unit, rank, and estimated Humanity
- A donor wall divided into human, animal, enemy, and uncertain material
- Resonance coils connected to field telephones
- Bone saws, pressure pumps, stitching frames, and memory cylinders
- A black-glass cistern that is never shown empty
- Incineration doors for contaminated tissue and permanently dead Battleborn
- A curtained gallery from which chaplains and officers observe

The workshop never becomes quiet. Pumps breathe. Teeth chatter in trays. Telephones ring without being answered. Something heavy moves in the donor wall when artillery lands.

## The Butcher's Work

Butchers do not restore a body to its original state. They make it capable of returning to duty. Their priorities are:

1. Recover military memory and useful reflexes.
2. Build enough structure to survive deployment.
3. Integrate available donor material.
4. Preserve personal identity when it does not interfere with the first three priorities.

A Butcher may be compassionate and still follow this order.

During reconstruction, the dead are not always unconscious. Muscle can react before identity returns. Donor parts may recognize a voice, resist a cut, or attempt a gesture from their previous life. Butchers restrain these movements without comment.

## The Price of Expertise

Every Butcher has witnessed thousands of people return incorrectly. They develop habits to remain functional:

- Referring to bodies by hook number until the patient speaks
- Never asking donor material where it came from
- Covering mirrors before facial reconstruction
- Humming over the sound of the other side
- Keeping one glove on even while sleeping
- Recording impossible memories as equipment faults

Some Butchers believe they are saving lives. Some believe they are maintaining weapons. The most frightening can no longer distinguish the two.

## Butchers and Sappers

A sapper depends on their Butcher and has every reason to fear them. The Butcher remembers bodies better than their owners do. They can identify an old rib beneath new skin, recognize which enemy supplied a hand, or casually mention that a heart has served in three different regiments.

Use a recurring Butcher to make revival personal. Give them one boundary they will not cross, then place the operation in a situation that pressures it.

Possible boundaries:

- Will not mix enemy brain tissue with an Allied face
- Will not revive anyone who asked for permanent death
- Will not open the sealed donor chamber below the workshop
- Will not perform a fourth reconstruction on the same skull
- Will not answer a field telephone during surgery

## Workshop Details

1. A tray of eyes turns toward the sapper whose body enters.
2. The Butcher recognizes a donor limb as their own former arm.
3. An assistant asks the dead sapper to confirm their name before the mouth is attached.
4. A gold-robed chaplain apologizes to the donor rather than the patient.
5. The saw stops when a disconnected telephone rings.
6. A corpse on the next table wakes with the sapper's childhood memory.
7. The Butcher removes an object from the chest that does not appear on any anatomical chart.
8. A labeled jar contains the identifying mark the player just obscured.
9. The replacement part grips the table hard enough to crack it.
10. The Butcher says, “This has rejected you before,” though the sapper has never seen it.
