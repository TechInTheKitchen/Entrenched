# The Allied Trench

The Allied trench is shelter, barracks, hospital, factory, church, prison, and slaughterhouse. It is the closest thing the sappers have to home, but that does not make it safe.

## Physical Layers

An established trench has grown downward over decades.

### Firing Level

The surface contains firing steps, observation slits, machine-gun nests, duckboards, ammunition lockers, and shallow shelters. Everything is wet, exposed, and repaired with whatever was available. Sandbags may contain earth, ash, hair, or rendered remains.

### Living Level

Below the firing line are bunks, kitchens, chapels, stores, signal offices, and command rooms. Privacy is created with blankets and rank. Lamps burn animal fat mixed with industrial oil. The trench smells of wet wool, disinfectant, cooked grain, cordite, and a sweeter odor coming from below.

### Recovery Level

Corpse hounds enter through gated ramps. Bodies are tagged, stripped, washed, and sorted. Equipment goes one direction. Recoverable human material goes another. The dead wait on hooks or shelves until the [[The Butchers]] have space.

### Deep Works

The oldest levels contain sealed galleries, abandoned chapels, failed workshops, cisterns, and tunnels that no current plan admits exist. Telephone crews sometimes hear conversations on disconnected wire. Butchers send assistants below for materials and refuse to say where the assistants found them.

## A Day Without Days

The trench has no natural morning. Shifts change according to whistles, bells, and bombardment schedules.

- Reveille counts who can stand, not who is alive.
- Inspection checks weapons, respirators, identifying tags, and signs that borrowed parts have begun acting independently.
- Meals are served when supply arrives. Meat is uncommon and never identified.
- Services are held before major assaults and after incinerations.
- The names of the dead are read only after command confirms they cannot return.

Soldiers sleep through artillery but wake when it stops. Silence usually means an assault, gas, or that the listener has gone deaf.

## People in the Trench

Use recurring figures sparingly. Familiar faces make later alteration matter.

- **The Map Clerk:** Copies player markings onto command maps and quietly preserves annotations that officers order erased.
- **The Quartermaster:** Knows which equipment returned without its bearer and which living weapons dislike particular sappers.
- **The Corpse-Hound Keeper:** Recognizes people by regimental scent after their faces cease to match.
- **The Chaplain:** Records original names in gold ink and battlefield names in black.
- **The Butcher's Assistant:** Is young enough to have been born after revival became ordinary.
- **The Replacement Officer:** Arrives with the same orders and voice as the previous officer but different hands.
- **The Trench Cook:** Remembers everyone's preferred meal even after they forget it themselves.

Give one of these figures a repeated habit. Later, transfer that habit to another body without explanation.

## Discipline

The Allied trench is an authoritarian military settlement. Orders are law because hesitation can expose thousands of people to artillery. That justification is real, and it is used to excuse everything.

Deserters are executed, reconstructed, and sent out again when recoverable. Repeated refusal may result in removal of useful parts before execution. Officers call this reassignment of material.

Punishment should remain background unless the players force it forward. The objective is to make deployment feel coerced without turning every trench scene into an argument with command.

## Short Trench Details

Choose one between runs:

1. A soldier practices a signature because their new hand writes another name.
2. A corpse hound scratches at a living sapper's bunk.
3. The soup contains a tooth bearing an gold filling.
4. Fresh uniforms are issued with old blood still inside the seams.
5. A child-sized respirator hangs above the chaplain's altar.
6. Two soldiers argue over which one owns the memory of the same wedding.
7. An officer's portrait has been altered to match their latest face.
8. A sealed recovery door knocks in the rhythm of the morning whistle.
9. The trench newspaper announces the capture of ground visible beyond the parapet in enemy hands.
10. A Butcher asks a sapper whether they still need a particular finger.
