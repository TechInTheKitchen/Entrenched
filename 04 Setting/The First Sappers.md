# The First Sappers

Official history says twelve Allied engineers crossed the wire in October 1916, captured an enemy reconstruction laboratory, and returned with the documents that became [[Project Eisenseele]]. Their sacrifice ended the enemy's monopoly on resurrection and saved the Allied cause.

The official history is printed on recruitment posters. The surviving records are less clean.

## The Resonance Raid

For several weeks in 1916, Allied observation posts reported the same enemy assault troops dying in daylight and returning before dawn. Artillery photographs showed bodies moving toward the enemy trench after bombardments. Telephone operators heard dead Allied soldiers speaking on enemy lines.

A sapper section was ordered to enter a buried structure beneath a ruined village and capture proof. Their names were later removed from the record and replaced by numbers One through Twelve.

The raid lasted eight days. Supply records show no food or ammunition delivered after the first night. Recovery logs claim seventeen Allied bodies returned.

## What They Brought Back

The First Sappers returned with:

- Surgical diagrams written in several languages and one unknown notation
- Glass ampoules containing Black Matter
- A captured technician who died repeatedly during interrogation
- A field telephone that rang when placed beside a corpse
- Seventeen sealed lead cases, although the departure inventory lists twelve
- Enough procedural knowledge to perform the first Allied revival

They did not return with an explanation.

## The First Allied Revival

The first official subject was Sapper Number Four, killed during the return crossing. The procedure restored breathing, movement, speech, and detailed military memory. Number Four correctly identified every member of the section.

Number Four also greeted three corpses by name. Those names appear nowhere in the departure record.

Within months, small reconstruction workshops operated along the front. Within a year, recovery doctrine treated bodies as strategic material. The surviving First Sappers became instructors, research subjects, saints, or sealed records depending on which archive is consulted.

## Contradictory Fates

Choose one account as true for an operation or let several remain possible:

1. All twelve eventually became the first Allied Battleborn and are entombed beneath the 187th's trench.
2. Only one person returned; the other bodies were copies carrying divided pieces of the same mind.
3. The captured enemy technician joined the section and invented the numbered identities to conceal it.
4. Colonel Vale is Number Four after hundreds of reconstructions.
5. The First Sappers never captured Project Eisenseele. Something below the village taught them how to make more bodies for it.
6. Every modern revival briefly passes through the memory of one First Sapper who never returned at all.

## Relics

First Sapper relics make useful operation hooks:

- A numbered identification disc that becomes warm near Black Matter
- An original map with trenches that do not exist yet
- A field notebook in the handwriting of a current sapper
- A preserved hand that points toward unrecovered bodies
- A sealed order naming the 187th decades before its formation
- A red flag made from an unknown uniform

Never settle every contradiction. The horror lies in the fact that revival works reliably while its origin remains fundamentally untrustworthy.
