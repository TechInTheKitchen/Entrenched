# The Long War

The Great War began in 1914. In every official calendar it is now 1951. Nobody at the front believes either number matters.

The opening years resemble the history old civilians still remember: mobilization, bright uniforms, cavalry dying beneath machine guns, and armies digging temporary trenches that became permanent borders. The promised breakthroughs never arrived. Governments fell without ending the war. New governments inherited the same trenches, renamed the same enemies, and conscripted another generation.

By 1951, western Europe is no longer a collection of recognizable nations. It is a belt of fortified states, military protectorates, ration territories, church administrations, and corpse-processing districts. Maps still carry old borders because printing new ones would imply that something had changed.

## Thirty-Seven Years Without Peace

The Long War did not remain technologically frozen. Everything useful to civilian life became scarce while anything that could move soldiers, preserve bodies, or break fortifications advanced rapidly.

- Railways feed ammunition, animals, recruits, and donor meat directly to the front.
- Cities manufacture standardized weapons but cannot keep their streetlights burning.
- Chemical plants produce gas, embalming agents, stimulants, and reconstruction fluids instead of fertilizer.
- Telephones connect officers across hundreds of miles but ordinary families wait years for censored letters.
- Medicine can return the dead to service but cannot promise a painless birth or cure common disease.

The war did not consume society. Society was rebuilt to feed the war.

## The Calcified Front

The Western Front has shifted only miles in decades. Some trenches have foundations, addresses, shrines, markets, schools, and cemeteries beneath their firing steps. Several have changed hands so often that their buried lower levels contain signs, bodies, and wiring from both sides.

Every captured trench is renamed. The old name survives in maintenance records, borrowed memories, and the mouths of enemy prisoners. A sapper may die defending a position their donor hand remembers constructing for the enemy.

Command measures success in yards, recovered equipment, and bodies denied to the opposing reconstruction workshops. Entire operations are fought to move an observation post far enough to see the next observation post.

## The Home Front

Few sappers have seen a true civilian district recently. Behind the lines, rationing begins at birth. Children learn gas alarms before arithmetic. Recruitment sermons play over factory horns. Families receive notification not only of death but of reuse: a stamped form may state that a loved one's remains were retained for continued national service.

The state promises that sacrifice preserves everyone behind the trench. It rarely explains how little remains behind it.

Letters from home are valuable because they prove a home existed. They are also dangerous. Many contain phrases copied by censors, news about people the recipient no longer remembers, or handwriting recognized as a replacement hand rather than its owner.

## What the Sappers Are Told

Every recruit is taught these facts:

1. The Allied line is the final barrier protecting human civilization.
2. The enemy began unlawful reconstruction and forced the Allies to answer in kind.
3. Revival returns the loyal dead to complete their duty.
4. The trenches of the hereafter await those permanently destroyed in service.
5. Victory remains possible if every soldier holds one day longer.

The first four claims contain useful pieces of truth. No living officer can prove the fifth.

## Referee Truths

- Both sides describe themselves as the Allies and the opposing force as the enemy.
- Both sides claim the other discovered reconstruction first.
- Civilian authority survives mostly as a vocabulary used by military administrations.
- Nobody commanding the present war remembers a world before it.
- The front persists partly because reconstruction makes casualties sustainable and partly because institutions built for war cannot imagine surviving peace.

Do not explain these truths in a lecture. Reveal them through an enemy identification disc stamped **ALLIED**, a captured prayer identical to one spoken in the players' trench, or a map on which the unit's homeland is labeled occupied territory.
