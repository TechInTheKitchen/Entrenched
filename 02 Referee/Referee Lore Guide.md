# Referee Lore Guide

The setting should feel larger than the operation without requiring the referee to explain thirty-seven years of alternate history. Lore appears as pressure, objects, habits, and contradictions.

## Five Firm Truths

1. The war has reorganized every institution around continuing itself.
2. Revival works, but nobody can prove what returns.
3. The enemy is horribly similar to the Allies.
4. No-man's-land remembers bodies, violence, and routes.
5. Becoming more useful means becoming less able to leave the war.

Everything else may be propaganda, doctrine, incomplete science, or a local truth.

## A Three-Step Reveal

For each operation, choose one lore thread and reveal it three times.

1. **Sign:** An unsettling detail with no explanation.
2. **Pattern:** A second detail showing the first was not accidental.
3. **Cost:** The truth changes a choice, body, route, or relationship.

Example: the unit finds an enemy hand wearing an Allied wedding ring; later an enemy prisoner recognizes the ring; finally a sapper's replacement hand remembers the prisoner's child.

## Horror Rhythm

Do not make every description wet flesh and screaming. Use contrast:

- Procedure followed by violation
- Familiarity followed by incorrect recognition
- Military efficiency applied to an impossible body
- Domestic comfort beside industrial death
- A useful adaptation accompanied by a private loss
- Silence after prolonged noise

One precise image is stronger than a paragraph of gore.

## Recurring Motifs

Choose two or three for an operation:

- Names crossed out or belonging to several bodies
- Hands performing work their owners never learned
- Telephone voices from disconnected lines
- Gold vestments stained progressively darker
- Red thread marking recoverable remains
- Teeth appearing in manufactured objects
- Maps that anticipate changes
- Animals remembering dead handlers
- Fire as both mercy and execution
- The smell of a home nobody present has visited

Repeat a motif with a changed meaning. A telephone voice may first warn the unit, later imitate them, and finally answer from inside a revived sapper.

## Questions to Ask Players

Use one after a revival or quiet discovery:

- Which familiar sensation now feels borrowed?
- What does the new part do while you sleep?
- Whose name almost comes when someone calls yours?
- What feature does your section use to recognize you now?
- What did you see on the other side that you refuse to describe?
- Which enemy position feels like somewhere you once lived?
- What ordinary object frightens the donor part?
- What promise are you no longer sure you made?

Accept short answers and reuse them later.

## Rumor Procedure

When the unit hears a rumor, decide secretly whether it is **true**, **partly true**, or **useful propaganda**. Do not announce the category. A false rumor should still reveal what the speaker fears or wants.

Rumors:

1. Colonel Vale has never died because there is no body inside the uniform.
2. Corpse hounds can smell the original soul beneath donor meat.
3. The enemy trench has been trying to surrender for eleven years.
4. The First Sappers returned with one more shadow than bodies.
5. Black Matter is leaking upward from the Final Trench.
6. Butchers keep the memories they remove and trade them as favors.
7. Every red flag raised on the front is the same flag.
8. Battleborn do not lose their humanity; they remember everyone else's.
9. A quiet sector exists where the dead refuse revival.
10. The war ended years ago, but neither front received the order.

## What Not to Explain

Never conclusively answer all of the following:

- Whether revival restores, copies, or replaces a person
- Whether the other side is an afterlife or a physical place
- What Black Matter ultimately is
- Which coalition began reconstruction
- Whether peace outside the military system is still possible

The players should understand procedures and consequences. They do not need a reliable cosmology.

## Connecting Lore to the Map

Every important piece of lore should eventually become spatial. Put its evidence somewhere the sappers can mark:

- A First Sapper recovery site
- A Black Matter seam
- A buried civilian home
- An enemy memorial
- A silent Project Eisenseele chamber
- A corpse route used by both sides

Lore becomes part of play when it changes where the unit goes, what it recovers, or what it chooses to destroy.
