# Retaliation Tables

These results extend the failure procedure in [[Skill Checks and Combat]]. Preserve lasting discoveries and changes using the map procedure in [[Core Game Loop]].

Outside combat, when a check fails or the unit hesitates, the battlefield retaliates. During combat, use these tables only when an action, hazard, or operation explicitly calls for an additional retaliation.

The referee may choose a fitting result or roll **three six-sided dice, add them together, and read the total** on the most relevant table. This produces results from 3 to 18.

The middle of each table is common: exposure, displacement, damaged equipment, approaching enemies, and survivable wounds. Results near 3 and 18 are increasingly rare and strange. A low extreme often reveals something useful through a dangerous accident; a high extreme permanently changes the map, destroys bodies, or brings overwhelming force.

| Total | Frequency |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3 or 18 | 0.5% each |
| 4 or 17 | 1.4% each |
| 5 or 16 | 2.8% each |
| 6 or 15 | 4.6% each |
| 7 or 14 | 6.9% each |
| 8 or 13 | 9.7% each |
| 9 or 12 | 11.6% each |
| 10 or 11 | 12.5% each |

Retaliation follows the fiction. Ignore or reinterpret a result that cannot happen in the current position. Shelter may prevent bombardment, an empty minefield cannot produce an enemy mine, and the enemy cannot react with knowledge nobody survived to report.

One result may threaten one sapper or the whole unit as written. If an attack threatens several targets, roll once and compare its successes separately with every target's Defense. Do not add unrelated punishments merely because the result is dramatic.

## Artillery and Mortars

| 3d6 | Retaliation |
| ---: | --- |
| 3 | A shell opens the battlefield instead of closing it: create a deep crater and reveal a buried entrance, body, cache, or Black Matter seam. Everyone within 2 squares is thrown 3 squares and loses one unsecured item into the new ground. |
| 4 | A massive unexploded shell buries itself nearby. Mark it as Resistance 2 to defuse or Resistance 3 to move safely; its casing bears coordinates for an unmarked enemy battery. |
| 5 | A body is thrown from old earthworks and lands 6 squares away carrying a recognizable map fragment or item. The impact reveals the unit's position. |
| 6 | Dense smoke and falling earth block sight beyond Close range until the unit or wind moves. Shapes inside the smoke move toward the nearest voices. |
| 7 | The blast throws every exposed sapper 2 squares in different directions and breaks the unit's formation. |
| 8 | One carried consumable loses a use, or one exposed item is torn loose and lands in an adjacent square. |
| 9 | A ranging shell lands beside the acting sapper. Mark the square; the next artillery retaliation centers there unless the unit changes position. |
| 10 | Shrapnel makes a 6-dice attack against the most exposed sapper. Its path reveals the direction of the firing battery. |
| 11 | Flying metal marks one exposed sapper's Flesh box; if none is empty, they become Dying. |
| 12 | Walking fire crosses the route. Every exposed sapper must immediately move 2 squares toward shelter or mark a Flesh box, becoming Dying if none is empty. |
| 13 | A shelter entrance collapses. Anyone inside remains protected but trapped; clearing the entrance is Resistance 2 Strength or Engineering. |
| 14 | The closest fallen body is thrown 6 squares and badly pulped. Its next field revival rolls twice on the Scar Table and keeps both scars. |
| 15 | A mortar crew brackets the area. Mark its center; until the unit escapes or silences the observers, hesitation produces two linked threats instead of one. |
| 16 | A direct hit destroys one marked piece of shelter. Occupants may flee immediately or be killed beneath it. Change the map permanently. |
| 17 | A heavy shell attacks every sapper in one chosen square with 10 dice and destroys every loose item and ordinary body there. |
| 18 | The bombardment erases a 2-square-radius area. Everyone inside is killed, ordinary bodies and equipment are destroyed, and a crater, fire, flooded pit, or Black Matter wound permanently replaces the terrain. |

## Scaling Retaliation

The dice already make ordinary battlefield pressure more common than extreme destruction. Do not add numerical modifiers to the 3d6 roll merely because the operation has progressed; doing so erases the usefulness of the curve and makes one extreme more common than the other.

Instead, scale through interpretation:

- Early in an operation, let common results reveal positions, alter movement, threaten equipment, and introduce enemies.
- As the unit learns the map, let the same results target established routes, scarce supplies, bodies, and recovery plans.
- Near the objective, let prepared enemy reactions overlap with whatever the roll creates.

Choose instead of rolling when only one consequence honestly follows from the fiction. Never protect a sapper merely because they have not died recently. Never kill them arbitrarily when a more specific consequence would make the map or next decision more interesting.

Brutality works best when players understand what killed them and carry that knowledge into the next run.
