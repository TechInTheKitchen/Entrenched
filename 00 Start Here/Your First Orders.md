# Your First Orders

Read this aloud together before creating sappers. When the text asks you to record something, pause and write it on the character sheet. By the time the truck reaches the 187th, the person on the sheet will be under military authority.

> [!NOTE] Before Induction
> Agree on boundaries using [[Safety and Tone]] before beginning. You may describe your sapper briefly, draw a veil over an image, or answer a prompt without explaining it. The induction establishes a character; it does not overrule anyone's limits.

## The Transport

You tremble as you share a packed transport truck with a dozen other recruits.

The canvas roof traps the smell of damp wool, old tobacco, and the sour antiseptic painted along the benches. Rifles knock together whenever the wheels enter a shell hole. Nobody acknowledges the sickly sweet smell of burnt flesh. Everyone holds their papers inside their coat as though the rain might dissolve their names before command has finished using them.

Beyond the canvas, western Europe passes in fragments: roofless villages, chalk roads, fields furrowed by artillery, and columns of soldiers moving toward a horizon that has flashed without pause since before you were born.

Someone near the tailgate asks how old you are. Before you answer, the truck stops.

Record your sapper's **name**, **date of birth**, **height and weight**, and one **identifying mark**. These details belong to the body that enlisted. Write them in ink. Do not leave room for corrections.

## What You Carried Here

The truck begins moving again.

You have been told not to bring personal effects. Nearly everyone has disobeyed. A recruit across from you holds a house key to a building that may no longer exist. Another wears a wedding ring on a cord because the proper finger was taken during a training incident. One has brought nothing and keeps checking their pockets anyway.

You try to remember the last place where the ground did not shake.

Record one **memory from before you joined the war**. Make it small enough to belong to one person: the color of a kitchen wall, somebody laughing with food in their mouth, wet shoes beside a stove, a particular hand resting on your shoulder. Do not write a history. Write something that would hurt to lose.

Then record one **person, place, or belief you must not forget**. This is not protection. It is the first entry the reconstruction clerks will compare against what returns.

## The Buried Regiment

Night has fallen when the truck enters the communications trench. There is no gate and no sign. The road simply sinks between walls of wet timber until the sky becomes a narrow black strip overhead.

Soldiers watch from firing steps as you pass. Some are bandaged. Some have shapes beneath their uniforms that no bandage would explain. A corporal raises a hand to stop the convoy, and the hand continues unfolding after an ordinary wrist should have ended.

Painted on the wall behind them is a plain red flag.

You have been assigned to the **187th Sapper Regiment**, formally recorded as the Royal 187th Regiment for Penetration, Recovery, and Fortification. Nobody says the full name. Here it is called the Buried Regiment, the Returned, or simply the One-Eighty-Seventh.

Its sections go where ordinary infantry cannot be spent efficiently. They locate mines, cross open ground, break fortifications, recover bodies, and prepare the route for everyone waiting behind them.

The regiment does not promise that you will survive. It promises that your service will continue.

## Classification

You leave the truck beneath a corrugated roof. A clerk takes your papers without looking at your face. Behind the desk, six boards list the work for which recruits are still required.

Choose one **Specialty**:

- **Scout:** You notice routes, movement, hidden positions, and danger before it reaches the section. Begin with Awareness 2.
- **Demolitionist:** You understand charges, blast direction, and which part of a structure must fail first. Begin with Demolitions 2.
- **Mine-Clearer:** You read disturbed earth, wire, mechanisms, and unexploded ordnance. Begin with Engineering 2.
- **Trench Fighter:** You clear confined positions and keep violence away from the sapper beside you. Begin with Close Quarters 2.
- **Runner:** You cross exposed ground, carry orders, and reach bodies while recovery is still possible. Begin with Mobility 2.
- **Rifleman:** You wait for a visible opening and kill at distance. Begin with Ranged 2.

Your Specialty grants its Field Doctrine from [[Specialties and Field Doctrines]]. It is the work command assigns you first, not the limit of what your body may eventually learn.

The clerk directs you to three testing stations. Nobody asks what you enjoy. They ask what you can already do while frightened, exhausted, and observed.

Assign **five additional skill ranks** among Ranged, Close Quarters, Awareness, Stealth, Engineering, Demolitions, Strength, and Mobility. Your Specialty already provides two ranks, giving you seven total. No skill may begin above rank 3.

When you finish, the clerk stamps the page:

> PRIVATE — HUMANITY 100 — DEFENSE 1 — FLESH 0

You are still entirely human. The regiment therefore expects a bullet to kill you quickly.

## Issue

The quartermaster works behind a mesh screen. Equipment hangs on numbered hooks. Every item is cleaner than the hands passing it to you.

You receive a steel helmet and quilted armor. The armor is meant to keep a body identifiable and prevent loose portions from being lost during transport. It does not raise your starting Defense above 1.

Choose **three level 1 items** from [[Equipment]]. A useful recruit normally carries:

- A weapon appropriate to their assigned work
- A tool that helps the section overcome an obstacle
- A second weapon, supply, explosive, or protective item

The quartermaster records each item's bearer. Command cares who was issued a thing because bodies return incomplete and expensive equipment does not always return with them.

## The Demonstration

Before you can leave, an orderly leads the recruits to a tiled room below the trench.

A dead sapper lies on the central table. Their tunic has been cut away. One boot is laced loosely around a leg that ends above the knee. A Butcher washes their hands in a metal basin and asks the orderly for a replacement by weight.

Nobody tells you to remain, but as your stomach turns your eyes remain fixed on the scene before you.

The Butcher fits the replacement into the body, tests the new joint, and closes the seam with black cord. With a soft utterance the corpse begins breathing, the recruits nearest the table step back. You do not. There is nowhere to step.

The revived sapper opens unmatching eyes and asks whether the red flag has risen.

The Butcher answers, "Not yet."

This is what command means by recovery. When you die and enough of you remains, another sapper may perform a field revival under fire, or the Butchers may reconstruct you after the corpse hounds bring you home. You will return with every Flesh box cleared. You will also lose Humanity, replace parts of your body, gain skill from what was fitted to you, and perhaps carry a memory filled scar belonging to the donor.

> [!NOTE] Loss on the battlefield
> Nothing is erased neatly. Your sheet will be crossed out, overwritten, and drawn upon as your sapper changes. The body diagram is not decoration. It is the service record command trusts most.

## Section Assignment

An officer divides the recruits into sections of three to six. You are placed with the other players.

Look at the sappers beside you. Say your sapper's name, Specialty, and the ordinary memory they wrote down. You do not need to trust one another yet. You need to know what voice to follow through smoke and which body must not be left for the enemy.

Together, confirm that every sheet has:

- A name and enlistment details
- One memory from before the war
- One person, place, or belief that must not be forgotten
- One Specialty and its Field Doctrine
- Exactly seven starting skill ranks, with no skill above 3
- Humanity 100, Defense 1, and no Flesh boxes
- Three level 1 items, plus issued helmet and armor
- An untouched body diagram and empty replacement record

If anything is missing, the clerk returns the page without comment, a cold look behind their eyes.

## The First Briefing

Your section is taken to a dugout where a paper map has been pinned beneath cracked glass. Most of no-man's-land is blank. Command has drawn the Allied trench, several distant landmarks, and the approximate location of an objective it considers essential.

Everything between those marks is your responsibility.

You will cross the parapet, discover what command does not know, and mark it on the shared map. Routes will remain useful. Destroyed positions will remain destroyed. Mines, shelters, patrols, bodies, and mistakes will accumulate in ink. The map may outlive every sapper who contributes to it.

Outside, infantry gather in the communications trenches. Gun crews wait beside loaded rifles and equipment stained brown and stitched together by unseen maintenance teams. Stretcher bearers arrange empty litters against the wall. None of them know when they will advance. They are waiting for your section to make the assault possible.

The officer folds a plain red flag and places it on the table.

"Your lives are replaceable," the officer says. "The operation is not."

Read [[Quick Reference]] or begin the briefing for [[Raise the Red Flag]]. When the Referee asks what your sapper does, answer in the first person if that feels natural.

You are no longer riding toward the Front.

You are part of it.
