# Safety and Tone

**Entrenched** is built around war, repeated death, surgery, bodily alteration, stolen memories, coercive authority, and loss of identity. These subjects should be intense for the characters without becoming harmful to the people playing them.

## Before Play

Tell everyone what the game contains. Discuss boundaries before characters or maps are prepared.

Use two lists:

- **Lines:** Content that will not appear at all.
- **Veils:** Content that may exist but will happen off-screen or without detailed description.

Ask specifically about:

- Surgical detail and medical trauma
- Eye, tooth, skin, limb, and organ horror
- Animal grafts and transformation
- Memory loss, dementia-like imagery, and identity erasure
- Cremation and permanent destruction
- Religious coercion and authoritarian punishment
- Harm involving children, civilians, or animals
- Starvation, gas, drowning, burial, and confinement

Nobody needs to explain a boundary.

## During Play

Any player may say **Pause** or make an agreed hand signal. Stop the description immediately. The group may revise the detail, veil the remainder of the scene, or move past it. Do not ask the player to defend the request.

Anyone may step away and return when ready. The game does not require a character explanation for a player's absence.

The referee can keep every mechanical consequence while veiling imagery. A reconstruction may still cost Humanity, alter the body record, and damage identity without describing a particular procedure.

## Character Control

Borrowed memories, donor impulses, and scar-memory intrusions create pressure but do not give the referee unlimited control of a player character. State the trigger and ask the player how the brief reaction appears. The player decides how their sapper yields or resists; an intrusion never dictates a lethal action, consumes an entire turn, or decides the character's loyalties.

Identity fields are physically obscured because Humanity is being lost. A player may choose how to mark the sheet and may use removable overlays, sticky notes, or a photocopy if permanent alteration of the original sheet would be uncomfortable.

## Horror, Not Humiliation

Describe bodies as altered, functional, intimate, and wrong. Do not treat disability, disfigurement, mental illness, nationality, or real-world ethnicity as shorthand for monstrosity. The horror comes from involuntary reconstruction, institutional exploitation, and foreign memories—not from a body failing to look ordinary.

The enemy may mirror the Allies without using real-world slurs or asking players to reproduce historical hatred.

## After Play

Take a few minutes to leave the trench behind. Ask:

- Is there imagery we should reduce or veil next time?
- Is there a moment whose consequences we want to preserve?
- Does anyone want a boundary changed?

Boundaries may change between or during sessions. Agreement at the beginning is not permanent consent.
