# Specialties and Field Doctrines

A specialty records the training a sapper received before first deployment. It grants 2 starting ranks in one skill and one **Field Doctrine**: a narrow technique that gives the specialty a distinct place in the unit without creating a separate subsystem.

A sapper keeps their Field Doctrine as their skills and body change. It is learned training, not a restriction. A Scout may eventually become the unit's strongest fighter, but they do not forget how to read exposed ground.

Unless a doctrine says otherwise:

- A doctrine may be used once per run.
- Using a doctrine is part of the action or event that triggers it and does not cost another action.
- A doctrine cannot make an impossible action possible, reveal information the sapper could not perceive, attack an unseen target, or bypass the Protection of a [[Stationary Defenses|stationary defense]].
- A use is spent when its effect is declared. If it modifies a check, it is spent whether that check succeeds or fails.
- A doctrine refreshes at the beginning of the next run.

## Scout: Read the Ground

Once per run, before the unit enters a visible area, the Scout may study it and ask the referee one question:

- What is watching this area?
- What is the safest visible approach?
- What here has changed since the map was made?
- Where could one sapper remain unseen?

The referee answers honestly with what the Scout can reasonably infer from their position. The answer may reveal a danger, firing lane, sign, or opportunity without explaining everything concealed there or how it must be defeated. Asking the question requires enough time and visibility to examine the area; it cannot interrupt an attack or other retaliation already being resolved.

## Demolitionist: Leave the Right Ruin

Once per run, after succeeding on a Demolitions check, the Demolitionist chooses one additional benefit that follows from the blast:

- One named nearby person, route, or object is spared.
- The wreckage creates useful cover.
- The destruction appears accidental until closely inspected.
- One useful piece of salvage survives and can be recovered.

The choice cannot contradict the declared purpose of the charge. A blast intended to collapse the only bridge cannot also preserve that bridge, and controlled destruction cannot overcome a fortification that has not yet been made vulnerable.

## Mine-Clearer: Follow My Steps

Once per run, after identifying a trapped, mined, wired, or unstable route, the Mine-Clearer may lead the unit through it. Make one Engineering check for the crossing.

On a success, every sapper who follows the Mine-Clearer's exact path as part of the same crossing passes through safely. On a failure, the crossing does not succeed and the referee makes one primary retaliation against the exposed unit rather than calling for a separate check or retaliation for every sapper.

This doctrine does not disarm the hazard or make the route permanently safe. Mark it as known but still dangerous on the shared map. Anyone who leaves the path or crosses it later faces the hazard normally.

## Trench Fighter: Behind Me

Once per run, when an adjacent sapper is selected as the target of an attack, the Trench Fighter may intervene before the attack is rolled.

If the attack has only one target, the Trench Fighter becomes its target. If the same attack already threatens both sappers, the protected sapper instead gains +1 Defense against that attack; the Trench Fighter gains no Defense. Resolve every other target normally.

The Trench Fighter must be able to perceive the attack and physically place their body between it and the protected sapper. This doctrine cannot intercept gas filling an area, collapsing ground, or another effect for which one body provides no protection.

## Runner: No One Left

Once per run, when another sapper becomes Dying, the Runner may immediately move up to their normal movement toward that sapper. Resolve this movement after the effect that made the sapper Dying and before play continues.

Every square crossed must bring the Runner closer by a viable route, and the movement must end closer to the fallen sapper. Dangerous ground still requires the normal check and may stop the Runner or provoke retaliation. Reaching the sapper does not include treatment, revival, an attack, or another free action.

## Rifleman: Wait for the Opening

The Rifleman may spend an action aiming at one visible position, route, or opening. Mark the Rifleman as **Aimed**. Their next Ranged attack against a target in that position reduces that target's Defense by 1, to a minimum of 1.

Unlike other doctrines, Wait for the Opening has no per-run limit because aiming costs an action. The Rifleman ceases to be Aimed if they move, attack another target, lose sight of the chosen position, or become Dying. The doctrine cannot attack an unseen target, ignore range, or bypass the Protection of a stationary defense. It may exploit an Opening that the unit has already created.

## Changing a Specialty Name

The player may rename a specialty to fit the sapper's history while retaining its skill and doctrine. A Scout might be called an Observer, a Runner a Stretcher-Bearer, or a Trench Fighter a Raider. The title changes; the rules do not.
