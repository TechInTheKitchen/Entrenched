# Repair Table

Every revival replaces something that could not be saved. Roll two ordinary six-sided dice and read them separately:

- The **Body Die** determines the replaced body part.
- The **Source Die** determines where the replacement came from.

Different-colored dice are convenient, but not required. If the dice match, roll the Body Die first and the Source Die second. Do not add them together. The result is a combination, not a total, so all 36 pairings are equally likely.

Record the body zone, exact donor, and visible alteration on the sapper's replacement record. Every revival also grants 1 universal skill rank as described in [[Death and Rebirth]]. The body result grants additional ranks, and Black Matter occasionally grants one more. A revival therefore grants an average of approximately 2.8 total skill ranks.

## The Body

The Body Die selects one anatomical category. An arm or leg result affects the side destroyed in the fiction. If the injury does not establish a side, the player chooses left or right. This choice is not another roll: it lets the drawing follow what happened to the sapper and helps spread later repairs across the body.

Mark the selected zone and draw the repair over it in permanent ink. Use fur, stitches, exposed bone, mismatched scale, donor markings, Black Matter, or whatever features make the source recognizable. A repair may include internal organs and surrounding tissue even when the exterior zone is narrow.

Never erase an earlier repair. When the same zone is replaced again, draw the new material over and through the old work. Extend it across one adjacent boundary without marking that neighboring zone as replaced. The changing silhouette is the most accurate portrait of the sapper.

When all ten zones have been marked, the sapper is **Totally Reconstructed**. Every later replacement must extend onto the written character record as well as the body outline. Draw stitches through a skill, cover part of an equipment box with fur, or let Black Matter run beneath the sapper's name. This is visual and permanent; it does not create another numerical bonus.

|   1 | Head and neck: skull, face, jaw, throat, and partial brain           | Gain 2 ranks divided among any skills. Obscure one additional word on the identity record and replace it with a donor thought. The sapper now remembers ideas that did not begin inside their skull. |
|   2 | Chest and upper torso: ribs, heart, lungs, back, and upper organs    | Gain 2 ranks divided among Ranged, Close Quarters, Strength, or Mobility. Breathing and heartbeat no longer keep a human rhythm.                                                                     |
|   3 | Left or right upper arm: shoulder, upper arm, and elbow              | Gain 1 rank in Ranged, Close Quarters, Demolitions, or Strength. The arm may reach or fold in ways the sapper does not expect.                                                                       |
|   4 | Left or right forearm and hand: wrist, fingers, and working surfaces | Gain 2 ranks divided among Ranged, Engineering, Demolitions, or Close Quarters. Borrowed reflexes begin familiar work before the sapper decides to move.                                             |
|   5 | Left or right upper leg: hip, thigh, and knee                        | Gain 2 ranks divided among Mobility, Stealth, or Strength. The sapper's gait changes to suit the donor.                                                                                              |
|   6 | Left or right lower leg and foot: shin, ankle, and toes              | Gain 1 rank in Mobility, Stealth, or Engineering. Tracks may no longer resemble a human soldier's.                                                                                                   |

Skill ranks gained from a replaced part must obey the maximum rank of 10. If every listed skill is already at rank 10, assign the rank to any other skill during the next between-runs step.

## Source Die: Replacement Source

| d6 | Source Family | Borrowed Impulse |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Laboring animal | A horse, ox, mule, or boar. Endure burdens, fight restraint, seek open ground, or answer pain with stubborn force. |
| 2 | Hunting or pack animal | A dog, wolf, corpse hound, or other predator. Follow scent, protect the pack, test weakness, and pursue what flees. |
| 3 | Human, unknown | Repeat habits without context and ache for a life whose names and loyalties are gone. |
| 4 | Human, Allied | Recall fragments of another sapper's service, friendships, orders, and death. |
| 5 | Human, enemy or civilian | Recognize enemy words, ordinary work, family, sacred places, or everything the battlefield consumed. |
| 6 | Black Matter | Gain 1 additional rank in any skill. The replacement desires stillness, darkness, burial, and a return to the other side. |

The source result names a family rather than one exact creature or person. Choose the precise donor from the remains available during the revival. If the unit rebuilds beside a dead corpse hound and rolls a 2, the replacement should probably come from that hound. If no obvious donor is present, the battlefield provides something buried, partial, or impossible to identify.

Borrowed impulses are primarily roleplaying prompts. After a failed check, revival, terror, or contact with something the donor remembers, the referee may bring an impulse into the sapper's perception. State the impulse and let the player decide how it appears. It does not erase a memory, cost Humanity, or take control unless another rule—such as a scar-memory intrusion—explicitly says so.

## Reading a Repair

Read the two dice together as one physical event.

For example, a Body Die of 4 and Source Die of 2 replaces one forearm and hand with material from a hunting or pack animal. If the sapper's right hand was destroyed beside a corpse hound, the player marks the **Right Forearm and Hand**, draws the graft, gains 2 ranks from the listed skills, and records a corpse-hound impulse involving scent, pursuit, or protection of the unit.

The same 4 and 2 rolled after a different death might use a wolf foreleg, a preserved military dog, or something that hunted in no-man's-land before it was given a name. The dice establish the structure; the battlefield establishes the horror.

## Repeated Replacement

A body zone may be replaced more than once. A repeated result does not restore a clean limb or organ. The new material is grafted onto whatever remains.

Each repeated replacement spreads visibly within the zone and across one adjacent boundary without marking that neighboring zone as replaced. An arm becomes a patchwork that creeps over the shoulder and across the chest. A replaced foot climbs the shin. New jawbone knots into an old skull. Fur, scars, donor meat, metal staples, and living tissue blend instead of forming neat boundaries.

Record every source that remains visible in the final part. All of their borrowed impulses remain with the sapper.

## Humanity and Borrowed Lives

Replacement impulses grow clearer as Humanity falls:

| Humanity | Experience of Borrowed Parts |
| --- | --- |
| 100–76 | Fleeting smells, phantom sensations, and dreams forgotten upon waking |
| 75–51 | Instinctive movements, recurring dreams, and emotional reactions without a known cause |
| 50–26 | Clear memories, recognizable desires, and reflexes that interrupt conscious thought |
| 25–1 | Donor instincts compete openly with the sapper's own identity and memories |
| 0 | The Battleborn can no longer reliably distinguish original memory from borrowed life |

The player decides how these impulses appear during ordinary play. The referee may bring them sharply into focus following failure, terror, revival, or contact with something the donor remembers.
