# Repair Table

Every revival replaces something that could not be saved. Roll `d100` and read the dice separately:

- The tens digit determines the replaced body part.
- The ones digit determines the source of the replacement.

Record both on the sapper's replacement record. A result of 00 means Hands replaced with Black Matter.

Every revival grants 1 universal skill rank as described in [[Death and Rebirth]]. The body-part result grants additional ranks. Including the occasional bonus from Black Matter, a revival grants an average of approximately 2.8 total skill ranks.

## The Body Record

The printed character sheet should include a human outline divided into six regions: **Head, Torso, Left Arm, Right Arm, Left Leg, and Right Leg**. Draw every repair directly over that outline in permanent ink. Use fur, stitches, exposed bone, mismatched scale, donor markings, Black Matter, or whatever features make the source recognizable.

Never erase an earlier repair. When the same region is replaced again, draw the new material over and through the old work. Extend it into an adjacent region as described under Repeated Replacement. The changing silhouette is the most accurate portrait of the sapper.

When all six regions have been marked, the sapper is **Totally Reconstructed**. Every later replacement must extend onto the written character record as well as the body outline. Draw stitches through a skill, cover part of an equipment box with fur, or let Black Matter run beneath the sapper's name. This is visual and permanent; it does not create another numerical bonus.

## Replaced Body Part

| Tens | Replaced Part | Effect |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 10 | Skull and partial brain | Gain 3 ranks divided among any skills. Obscure one additional word on the identity record and replace it with a donor thought. The sapper now remembers ideas that did not begin inside their skull. |
| 20 | Face, scalp, jaw, and neck | Gain 1 rank in Awareness, Stealth, Mobility, Ranged, or Close Quarters. Obscure one feature the sapper used to recognize in a mirror. Familiar voices become difficult. |
| 30 | Upper torso, heart, and lungs | Gain 2 ranks divided among Ranged, Close Quarters, Strength, or Mobility. Breathing and heartbeat no longer keep a human rhythm. |
| 40 | Lower torso, gut, and back | Gain 2 ranks divided among Strength, Mobility, Engineering, or Demolitions. Hunger may belong to more than one body. |
| 50 | Thighs, hips, and knees | Gain 2 ranks divided among Mobility, Stealth, or Strength. The sapper's gait changes to suit the donor. |
| 60 | Shins, ankles, and feet | Gain 1 rank in Mobility, Stealth, or Engineering. Tracks may no longer resemble a human soldier's. |
| 70 | Shoulders, spine, and neck | Gain 2 ranks divided among Strength, Mobility, Engineering, or Demolitions. The replacement may alter posture, balance, and the shape beneath a uniform. |
| 80 | Upper arms and elbows | Gain 1 rank in Ranged, Close Quarters, Demolitions, or Strength. The arms may reach or fold in ways the sapper does not expect. |
| 90 | Forearms and wrists | Gain 1 rank in Ranged, Engineering, or Demolitions. Borrowed reflexes remain in the fingers. |
| 00 | Hands | Gain 2 ranks divided among any skills. The hands sometimes begin familiar work before the sapper decides to move them. |

Skill ranks gained from a replaced part must obey the maximum rank of 10. If every listed skill is already at rank 10, assign the rank to any other skill.

## Replacement Source

| Ones | Source | Borrowed Impulse |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Boar | Dig in, charge the threat, and answer pain with fury. |
| 2 | Horse | Run with others, fear confinement, and recoil from the smell of blood. |
| 3 | Dog | Follow the familiar, protect the pack, and recognize people by scent. |
| 4 | Wolf | Test weakness, circle danger, and hunger beside the pack. |
| 5 | Deer | Freeze beneath observation, bolt at sudden violence, and listen for distant movement. |
| 6 | Human, unknown | Repeat habits without context and ache for a life with no name. |
| 7 | Human, Allied | Recall fragments of another sapper's service, loyalties, and death. |
| 8 | Human, enemy | Recognize enemy words, positions, and faces with unwanted familiarity. |
| 9 | Human, civilian | Remember warmth, ordinary labor, family, and everything the battlefield consumed. |
| 0 | Black Matter | Gain 1 additional rank in any skill. The replacement desires stillness, darkness, and a return to the earth. |

Borrowed impulses are primarily roleplaying prompts. After a failed check, the referee may use an impulse to shape retaliation: a horse leg bolts from fire, an enemy hand reaches toward a familiar uniform, or a wolf jaw refuses to release the dead. The consequence should follow the situation and should not erase the player's control without warning.

## Repeated Replacement

A body part may be replaced more than once. A repeated result does not restore a clean limb or organ. The new material is grafted onto whatever remains.

Each repeated replacement spreads into an adjacent area. An arm becomes a patchwork that creeps over the shoulder and across the chest. A replaced foot climbs the shin. New jawbone knots into an old skull. Fur, scars, donor meat, metal staples, and living tissue blend instead of forming neat boundaries.

Record every source that remains visible in the final part. All of their borrowed impulses remain with the sapper.

## Humanity and Borrowed Lives

Replacement impulses grow clearer as Humanity falls:

| Humanity | Experience of Borrowed Parts |
| --- | --- |
| 100–76 | Fleeting smells, phantom sensations, and dreams forgotten upon waking |
| 75–51 | Instinctive movements, recurring dreams, and emotional reactions without a known cause |
| 50–26 | Clear memories, recognizable desires, and reflexes that interrupt conscious thought |
| 25–1 | Donor instincts compete openly with the sapper's own identity and memories |
| 0 | The Battleborn can no longer reliably distinguish original memory from borrowed life |

The player decides how these impulses appear during ordinary play. The referee may bring them sharply into focus following failure, terror, revival, or contact with something the donor remembers.
