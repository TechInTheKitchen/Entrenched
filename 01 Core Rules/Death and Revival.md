# Death and Revival

See [[Combat and Movement]] for lethal hits, Wounds, and Dying; [[Repairs and Scars#Repair Table|Repair Table]] for replacement parts; and [[Repairs and Scars#Scar Table|Scar Table]] for the consequences of field revival.

For a sapper, death is not the end of service. Bodies can be recovered, repaired, and sent back across no-man's-land. Something is lost during every death, however, and no reconstruction returns exactly the person who fell. Track that loss in [[Humanity and Battleborn]].

## Death and Wounds

Combat is lethal, but repeated reconstruction teaches a body to remain alive after wounds that should kill it. When an attack meets a sapper's Defense, it inflicts a **lethal hit**. Mark an empty Wound box and become Wounded. If no empty box remains, the sapper becomes **Dying** instead.

A Dying sapper remains alive until their next sapper turn. They take one Last Action with every penalty from marked Wound boxes, then die. They cannot be treated out of this state or begin a field revival. Another lethal hit or an effect that explicitly kills them ends them immediately.

Non-combat retaliation may instead leave a sapper **Wounded** when immediate death does not fit the danger. Wounds represent injuries that remain survivable only because the sapper is still moving: shattered bones, torn muscle, burns, embedded shrapnel, damaged organs, or similar trauma.

A wounded sapper is alive and may continue the run, but every marked Wound box reduces their checks and attacks by 1 die. Environmental retaliation can also mark a Wound box. If there is no empty box when an effect would mark one, the sapper becomes Dying unless the effect explicitly kills them. Treatment can clear marked boxes but cannot give a sapper more boxes than their current Humanity allows. The exact effects and condition definitions are covered in [[Combat and Movement]].

### Tracking Wounds at the Table

The printed Wound spaces show **capacity**, not permanent injuries: none at 100–76 Humanity, one at 75–26, and two at 25–0. Label two spots on the edge of the character sheet **Wound 1** and **Wound 2**. Put a red paper clip at an available spot whenever a Wound box is marked; each clip is one current wound and costs 1 die. Remove a clip when treatment clears that wound or when revival clears all Wound boxes. Do not write on or erase the printed Wound spaces to track temporary wounds. A printed space that the sapper has not yet gained from Humanity cannot absorb a hit.

## Field Revival

If at least one member of the unit survives a fight or disaster, the survivors may revive their fallen comrades in the field. They stitch together damaged bodies with nearby remains, spare parts, and whatever equipment they carried into no-man's-land.

Enough of the body must remain to identify and rebuild the sapper. The reviver must be in the same or an adjacent square. Nearby donor material is assumed unless the fiction has explicitly removed or destroyed it. Revival requires no check.

Reviving one body takes the sapper's full turn. The reviver must remain beside the fallen sapper through the following enemy turn and can do nothing else. If the reviver survives and is not forced away, the fallen sapper returns at the beginning of the next sapper turn. Every fallen sapper requires a separate revival.

The revived sapper returns with every Wound box cleared. They may act normally during the sapper turn in which they return. Apply Humanity loss and every threshold crossed immediately; a Wound box gained at a new threshold begins empty. Roll and record the repair and scar immediately. Equipment still present on the body remains with its bearer.

A field-revived sapper:

- Returns during the current run.
- Loses `1d6 + 2` Humanity.
- Rolls on the [[Repairs and Scars#Repair Table|Repair Table]] to determine what was replaced and its source.
- Rolls on the [[Repairs and Scars#Scar Table|Scar Table]] and gains 1 scar.
- Gains 1 universal skill rank to assign before the next run, in addition to ranks granted by the replacement.

Field revival costs less Humanity than reconstruction by the Butchers. The sapper spends less time dead, less time witnessing the other side, and less time forgetting the person they were before death. The price is an improvised body assembled under fire.

Each scar combines a permanent physical alteration with a borrowed echo. No more than three scars are Active during a run and able to provide their adaptations, but every scar remains visible and capable of intrusion. When an echo encounters a meaningful trigger, it tries to replace one of the sapper's written memories; resisting it costs Humanity. Scars are not merely wounds: they are useful defects, mismatched instincts, and visible evidence that the sapper was rebuilt incorrectly.

## Recovery by the Butchers

If nobody remains to recover and revive the unit, [[Corpse Hounds and Handlers|corpse hounds]] or another sapper detail eventually bring the bodies back to the Allied trench. There, the [[The Butchers]] reconstruct the fallen with proper tools and the best available parts.

>[!note] **A Corpse Hound and it's handler ready for a recovery mission**
>![[corpse hound and handler.webp]]

A sapper revived by the Butchers:

- Returns before the next run.
- Loses `2d6 + 2` Humanity.
- Rolls on the [[Repairs and Scars#Repair Table|Repair Table]] to determine what was replaced and its source.
- Gains 1 universal skill rank to assign before the next run, in addition to ranks granted by the replacement.

The Butchers produce cleaner joins and do not inflict a field scar by default, but the longer death takes a greater portion of the sapper's Humanity and gives the workshop time to cut away pieces of identity. During every Butcher revival, complete the Butcher's procedure below.

## The Butcher's Procedure

Do not skip from death directly to restored statistics. The player should feel the workshop reclaiming their sapper.

1. The Butcher names the part that cannot be saved. The player marks it permanently on the body record before the replacement is rolled.
2. Roll the replacement and donor source. Describe the material laid beside the opened body: its temperature, smell, movement, or remaining evidence of its former life.
3. The player describes one moment their sapper remembers from the table: a saw beginning, a hand inside the chest, the sound of bone being tested, or the first sensation carried by the donor part.
4. Apply Humanity loss and any identity threshold crossed. Use a dark marker to obscure the affected information. It may be crossed out or written over, but never erased cleanly.
5. The sapper wakes and answers one question: **What no longer feels as though it belongs to you?**

Keep this scene short. One sharp sensory detail and one damaged memory are more effective than a long surgical account. Players may draw a veil over specific imagery while still applying every mechanical and character-sheet consequence.

## Death Under Orders

The body learns only from a death suffered while pursuing the operation under genuine threat. A death deliberately arranged to obtain replacement material or skill ranks grants **no universal revival rank and no ranks from the replacement**. Execution by an ally, controlled self-destruction, and a revival staged safely behind the line are not training.

> [!NOTE] Referee Judgment: Staged Death
> The revival, Humanity loss, replacement, scar, and every other consequence still occur. Invoke this rule only when gaining ranks was plainly the purpose of the death. Desperate sacrifices, failed plans, and calculated battlefield risks remain legitimate service.

## No Sapper Left Behind

The unit's objectives demand cohesion. Bodies are not dragged during ordinary play. Survivors must field-revive a fallen sapper before retreating together. A body left behind is abandoned and resolved after the run: Allied corpse hounds may recover it for Butcher reconstruction, the enemy may capture it, or an established battlefield threat may destroy or transform it.

Abandoning a recoverable Allied body costs Humanity as listed in [[Humanity and Battleborn#Losing Humanity in No-Man's-Land|Losing Humanity in No-Man's-Land]]. The referee states the body's likely fate before the survivors commit to retreat whenever their sappers could judge it.

The enemy normally kills, burns, strips, or ignores human-looking prisoners. It attempts capture for study when a sapper has **25 Humanity or less**, visibly carries extensive reconstruction, or possesses a level 3 item. A mission may establish another specific reason for capture. Captured Battleborn suffer the [[Humanity and Battleborn#Burn Boxes|Burn rule]].

Capture is not immediate permanent death. Whenever a rescue is still possible, mark the captive's location or likely route and give the unit a concrete chance to recover them during the current or next run. A captive remains that player's sapper while rescue is possible, though the player may temporarily portray an Allied recruit, corpse hound, or another willing player's intrusive memory. If the rescue window closes and the captive is destroyed, transported beyond the operation, or otherwise made unrecoverable, resolve permanent death.

Leaving a fallen or captured sapper behind should always be a last resort. The referee should show the cost, the likely enemy route, and at least one dangerous recovery opportunity when the fiction permits it. Recovery can become a sub-objective that competes with the primary mission rather than being erased between runs.

## Permanent Death and Replacement Recruits

When one sapper dies permanently but the unit remains in the operation, that player may create a new Private at the next return to the Allied trench. The recruit begins with the normal starting profile and inherits the unit's shared map, but none of the dead sapper's Humanity, skill ranks, scars, repairs, memories, military rank, or claim to scarce equipment.

A replacement recruit keeps the player in the operation without making permanent death cheap. The empty bunk, overwritten sheet, unrecovered body, and knowledge preserved on the map remain evidence of the sapper who was lost.

The operation ends when the unit completes its mission or every remaining sapper is permanently destroyed.
