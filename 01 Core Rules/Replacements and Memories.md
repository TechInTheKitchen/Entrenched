# Replacements and Memories

Revival replaces the parts ruined by the fatal injury, using material the unit can actually recover. **Choose the body part from the fatal injury and the source from the available remains. Roll only when neither the scene nor a quick table decision can settle the missing detail.** See [[Death and Revival]] for revival timing and recovery.

## Resolve a Replacement

1. **Name the damage.** Identify the part the fatal injury destroyed or made unusable.
2. **Name the donor.** Use suitable remains or supplies already present. The player chooses among available sources; the referee confirms what can be used.
3. **Pay and record.** Apply the normal revival Humanity loss, plus 2 if Black Matter replaces the part. Note the part, source, and ranks owed, clear Wounds, and resume play.

A short note is enough during danger: **“Right hand, corpse hound; 2 ranks.”** Finish the drawing and assign the ranks. A borrowed memory is optional and can be added later without delaying revival.

### Skill Ranks

Every eligible revival grants **2 skill ranks total**, divided among any skills. If the replacement uses Black Matter, gain **1 additional rank, for 3 total**. 

One revival grants one package of ranks, even if the injury requires rebuilding several parts or using several donors. Any Black Matter replacement makes that package 3 ranks; it never adds more than one bonus rank.

Assign ranks during the next between-runs step. Each rank checks one box; no skill may exceed 12 checked boxes. Choose other skills if needed, and discard ranks only if every skill is full. A death staged solely to gain ranks grants none, including no Black Matter bonus. All revival costs still apply.

### Black Matter

Black Matter must be present in recoverable material, carried supplies, or an established workshop stock. Wanting its bonus does not make it available. Tell the player before they choose it: **3 ranks total and 2 extra Humanity lost.**

Add the 2 Humanity to that revival's cost before applying thresholds: **field revival costs `1d6 + 4`; Butcher revival costs `2d6 + 4`.** This extra loss applies every time Black Matter is used, outside the incidental Humanity-loss limit. Do not also charge the incidental Black Matter-contact cost for the same graft. 

Trace Black Matter involved in ordinary reconstruction does not count. The bonus and extra loss apply only when Black Matter is used as the replacement material. Using several Black Matter parts in one revival still adds only 1 rank and 2 Humanity loss.

## The Body

Let the death determine the replacement. A crushed hand needs a new hand; a shot through the chest needs new chest tissue or organs. Do not roll a leg merely because a table is available when the sapper died from a head wound.

For a blast, fire, drowning, or another death with several plausible injuries, briefly choose the damaged part that best fits what happened. Follow an established left or right injury; if the side is unknown, the player chooses. Internal damage can be recorded in the corresponding body zone.

Choose the source separately from the injury. A sapper killed by a wolf does not automatically receive wolf tissue unless the wolf's remains are available. A dead corpse hound beside the body, a fallen soldier, or a workshop's donor stock supplies an immediate answer.

## Repair Table

These are **last-resort tables**, not a step in every revival. If the part is known, do not roll for it. If the source is known or the players can choose among usable donors, do not roll for it. Roll only the detail nobody can otherwise establish.

### Fallback Body Parts

Roll one d6 only when the fatal injury and a quick choice leave the damaged part unresolved.

|  d6 | Replaced part                                |
| --: | -------------------------------------------- |
|   1 | Head or neck                                 |
|   2 | Chest and abdomen, including internal organs |
|   3 | Right arm, upper or lower                    |
|   4 | Left arm, upper or lower                     |
|   5 | Right leg, upper or lower                    |
|   6 | Left leg, upper or lower                     |

### Fallback Sources

If ordinary donor material is assumed but nobody can identify it, roll one d6. This establishes what the reviver finds among otherwise unspecified remains.

|  d6 | Source                                                                                          |
| --: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   1 | Draft animal                                                                                    |
|   2 | Hunting or pack animal                                                                          |
|   3 | Human, unknown                                                                                  |
|   4 | Human, Allied                                                                                   |
|   5 | Human, enemy or civilian                                                                        |
|   6 | Black Matter, if its presence is plausible and has not been ruled out; otherwise human, unknown |

A fallback result cannot create material the scene explicitly lacks. If no usable donor material remains, the unit must obtain it or recover the sapper for the Butchers; rolling cannot bypass that obstacle.

## Recording the Body

Record the replaced zone, donor source, and one visible alteration. Use stitches, donor markings, mismatched proportions, fur, or Black Matter to show what changed. Detailed drawing can wait until between runs.

Keep the earlier alterations visible when a part is replaced again. If the body diagram fills, continue on the replacement record or a spare sheet. Recording a repair adds no separate bonus, penalty, or mandatory drawing procedure.

### Repeated Replacement

A body zone may be replaced more than once. The new repair follows the new injury and available source. It grants the same 2 ranks, or 3 for Black Matter, as any other eligible revival.

For existing sappers, keep previously earned ranks and recorded alterations. Retire former adaptation bonuses and memory costs; do not recalculate ranks earned before this revision.

## Borrowed Memories

A replacement may bring a smell, habit, image, or feeling from its donor. These are **optional roleplay aids**. The player may use, change, or ignore them. They never add dice, change Defense, clear wounds, force an action, erase a written memory, or cost Humanity.

The referee may offer a prompt when it fits a quiet moment. There are no triggers to track, no Active choices, and no required memory roll. Humanity thresholds still change the identity record as described in [[Humanity and Battleborn]]; a roleplay prompt does not cause those changes.

Choose a detail from the donor, or use one of these twelve examples without rolling. Adapt any detail to suit the donor:

| Source                   | Optional memory or habit                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Draft animal             | A warm palm resting between your eyes while someone loosens a muddy harness. You remember the touch, but not the face.          |
| Draft animal             | The sweet taste of an apple offered over a fence, and the sound of a child laughing when you take it.                           |
| Hunting or pack animal   | Sleeping against the warm flank of another animal while rain drums on the kennel roof.                                          |
| Hunting or pack animal   | A two-note whistle across a field. You remember running toward it, certain someone would be waiting.                            |
| Human, unknown           | A chipped blue cup on a kitchen table. You remember which way its handle faced, but nothing beyond the table.                   |
| Human, unknown           | Someone braiding your hair before dawn. Their fingers pause whenever they laugh.                                                |
| Human, Allied            | Losing the same brass button in a card game three nights running. The winner always gave it back before inspection.             |
| Human, Allied            | A friend making up filthy words to a marching song. You remember the whole verse, but the friend's name is gone.                |
| Human, enemy or civilian | Rocking a child to sleep with a lullaby in a language you cannot otherwise speak.                                               |
| Human, enemy or civilian | Flour worked into the cracks of your hands, a hot oven at your back, and someone calling that the first customers have arrived. |
| Black Matter             | Lying beneath the earth while roots grow through you. Whole seasons pass between the footsteps overhead.                        |
| Black Matter             | Standing in a trench with no sky. Far away, someone calls your name in your own voice.                                          |

One brief detail is enough. A memory can return in conversation or color a decision without becoming another rule to resolve.

## Example

A mine destroys Bell's left lower leg beside a dead corpse hound. The unit uses the hound's remains. No body-part or source roll is needed. Bell's field revival costs `1d6 + 2` Humanity, clears his Wounds, and grants 2 ranks to assign between runs. His player notes the graft and may later describe a remembered whistle.

If the only usable material is an established Black Matter seam, the same revival instead costs `1d6 + 4` Humanity and grants 3 ranks total. The injury still determines the replaced leg.
