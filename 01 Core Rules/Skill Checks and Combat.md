# Skill Checks and Combat

Skill checks and attacks use the same basic rule: gather a pool of six-sided dice, roll them, and count every 6.

Each 6 is a **success**. If the roll produces successes equal to or greater than the target number, the action succeeds. Otherwise, it fails. Entrenched does not use partial successes. War does not take half measures.

## Building a Dice Pool

When a sapper attempts a dangerous action, build their dice pool from:

> Relevant skill + relevant equipment + other modifiers

A sapper with Engineering 3 and a mine-clearance kit rated 2 rolls five dice to disarm a mine.

If a sapper has no ranks in the required skill, they may still attempt the action with one die. Equipment and other modifiers are then added to that die as normal.

Only roll when failure would expose the unit to danger or change the situation. Ordinary actions performed without pressure do not require checks.

## Resistance

Tasks and battlefield hazards have **Resistance**. Resistance is the number of successes required to overcome them.

| Resistance | Opposition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dangerous | A trained sapper has a fair chance; cross open ground between bursts or notice a poorly concealed mine |
| 2 | Severe | Requires a strong, well-equipped sapper; defuse an unfamiliar mine or pass an alert patrol unseen |
| 3 | Extreme | Usually requires a strong sapper, proper equipment, and cooperation; breach a reinforced position under fire |
| 4+ | Operation | Cannot normally be overcome in one unsupported roll; requires preparation, several sappers, or progress across earlier runs |

Because only sixes count, each increase in Resistance is substantial. Do not use Resistance 2 for routine professional work or Resistance 3 merely because something sounds difficult. Resistance 3 is a unit problem.

The referee tells the players the Resistance before they commit to an action whenever the danger would be apparent to their sappers. Hidden threats may conceal their Resistance until discovered.

If the roll meets or exceeds the Resistance, the sapper accomplishes what they attempted. Extra successes do not normally create a greater effect unless a rule or piece of equipment says otherwise.

If the roll fails, the enemy or battlefield immediately retaliates. The action does not succeed, and the referee introduces a consequence that follows from the danger: incoming fire, a mine detonation, an alerted patrol, lost equipment, collapsing cover, or another lethal development.

Retaliation may threaten the acting sapper, another exposed sapper, or the entire unit. The fiction determines who is in danger.

During combat, a failed attack or check does not create an extra immediate retaliation unless the action, item, hazard, or operation explicitly says it does. The action still fails, and the enemy acts on its normal turn. This prevents one failed action from giving the enemy both an interruption and its full turn.

## Working Together

Two or more sappers may work together when they can meaningfully contribute to the same action. Choose one sapper to lead. The leader builds the normal pool from skill, one relevant item, and other modifiers. Every helper must have at least one rank in the required skill and adds only their skill dice. Helpers do not add equipment or other modifiers.

Roll all contributed dice together and compare the combined successes to the task's Resistance. One item cannot benefit several participants in the same check.

Cooperation commits everyone involved. If the check fails, every participating sapper is exposed to the resulting retaliation when the fiction allows it.

During combat, leading or helping with a cooperative check consumes that sapper's action for the turn. A helper cannot contribute skill dice and then take another action in the same sapper turn.

**Resistance 2 example:** A mine-clearer leads with Engineering and a mine detector while another trained sapper adds only their Engineering ranks to hold exposed wiring steady.

**Resistance 3 example:** A demolitionist leads a breach with Demolitions and shaped charges while two trained sappers contribute their Demolitions ranks to brace the charge and identify the bunker supports.

### Operation-Level Resistance

Resistance 4 or higher represents a problem that cannot normally be solved by accumulating successes on repeated rolls. Discoveries, sabotage, equipment, and completed sub-objectives reduce its Resistance or remove a prerequisite before the decisive check.

For example, a bunker begins at Resistance 4 and cannot be breached directly. Cutting its reinforcement tunnel reduces it to 3; detonating its ammunition cache reduces it to 2. Failed preparation checks still provoke retaliation, but another route or later run remains possible.

When one tool or non-combat action tries to affect several targets or a much larger area, the referee may increase its Resistance when the wider effect is genuinely harder. Do not increase Resistance merely because several people benefit from one successful action.

## Wounds

Humanity determines how many **Flesh boxes** a sapper has: none above 75 Humanity, one at 75, and two at 25. When an attack meets the sapper's Defense, it inflicts a lethal hit. If the sapper has an empty Flesh box, mark it and the sapper becomes Wounded. If no empty box remains, the sapper becomes **Dying**.

A Dying sapper is down but not out. They remain where they fell. On their next sapper turn, they may take one **Last Action**, applying all penalties from marked Flesh boxes, and then die immediately. The Last Action may be any action that can be completed at once, but it cannot begin field revival. A Dying sapper cannot be treated or returned to ordinary Wounded status. Another lethal hit or an effect that explicitly kills them ends them immediately, before their Last Action.

If a sapper becomes Dying during the sapper turn, their Last Action occurs on the following round's sapper turn. If they become Dying during the enemy turn, it occurs on the immediately following sapper turn. Becoming Dying while resolving an action does not grant a second action in the same turn.

Non-combat retaliation may also mark a Flesh box. If the sapper has no empty box, the retaliation leaves them Dying unless it explicitly says they are killed.

Every marked Flesh box removes 1 die from every check and attack. Dice pools cannot be reduced below one die from wounds. Treatment clears one marked box. Reconstruction clears every marked box, but never gives more boxes than current Humanity allows.

Rules use two distinct terms:

- A **lethal hit** marks an empty Flesh box; when none remains, it leaves the sapper Dying.
- An effect that says a sapper is **killed** bypasses Flesh boxes. It represents complete destruction, crushing, drowning, direct shell impact, or another consequence reconstructed toughness cannot soften.

The referee should make an obvious killing danger clear before it is resolved. Do not replace ordinary attacks with automatic killing effects.

## Conditions

- **Exposed:** Visible and reachable by the current attack or danger. Cover matters by preventing exposure, not by raising Defense.
- **Wounded:** Alive with one or more marked Flesh boxes. Each marked box removes 1 die from checks and attacks.
- **Dying:** Out of ordinary action after suffering a lethal hit with no empty Flesh box. The sapper receives one Last Action on their next turn, then dies.
- **Fallen:** Dead, present on the battlefield, and potentially recoverable.
- **Destroyed:** Dead with too little intact material for immediate revival. Recovery requires whatever remains or a specific fictional solution.
- **Captured:** Held or carried away by the enemy. The enemy normally studies only sappers at 25 Humanity or less, visibly extensive reconstructions, or bearers of level 3 equipment. A captured Battleborn fills every remaining Burn box.
- **Surprised:** Unaware when combat begins. Defense is reduced by 1, to a minimum of 1, through the first round.
- **Permanently dead:** Unable to return because the body is unrecoverable, the third Burn box is marked, or an effect explicitly prevents reconstruction.

## Resolution Order

When several effects follow one roll, resolve them in this order:

1. Determine success or failure, including any allowed reroll.
2. Apply the action's effect on a success or the primary retaliation on a failure.
3. Resolve attacks, marked Flesh boxes, Dying, or explicit killing.
4. Resolve one triggered scar-memory intrusion when applicable.
5. Damage, destroy, or separate equipment when the consequence calls for it.
6. Apply Humanity loss and immediately resolve any threshold crossed.
7. Update positions, bodies, hazards, and discoveries on the map.

One failed check normally creates one primary retaliation. A scar, item, or named hazard may add its stated consequence, but failure is not permission to pile on unrelated punishments. A successful check accomplishes its declared purpose before any stated cost is applied.

## Combat

Combat is divided into rounds. Each round has two turns:

1. **Sapper Turn:** Every living sapper may act once, in any order the players choose.
2. **Enemy Turn:** Every formation, independent enemy, and monster may act once, in any order chosen by the referee.

After the enemy turn, begin a new round. Continue until one side is dead, retreats, surrenders, or is otherwise unable to fight.

Failed attacks and checks do not cause an additional immediate retaliation during combat unless a specific rule says otherwise. Once combat begins, the enemy turn is the retaliation. Enemies act every round whether the sappers' actions succeed or fail.

### Surprise

If combat begins before one side detects the other, the unaware side is **Surprised** for the first round. A Surprised combatant has −1 Defense, to a minimum of Defense 1, until the end of that round. The Surprised side still acts on its normal turn. Once any member of a formation detects the attack, the whole formation is no longer Surprised after the first round.

Concealment, a silent approach, a prepared ambush, or a scenario rule may create Surprise. Merely acting first does not. State who is Surprised before the first attack is rolled.

## Position and Range

Combat uses squares on the operation map or a quickly sketched local map. Distance is divided into three practical ranges:

- **Melee:** An adjacent square.
- **Close:** From 2 to 6 squares away, suited to pistols, shotguns, thrown weapons, and other short-ranged attacks.
- **Far:** 7 or more squares away, suited to rifles, machine guns, and other long-ranged attacks.

Cover does not increase Defense against direct attacks. A firefight is too fast, violent, and confused for a sapper to rely on a wall staying between them and a bullet. Substantial shelter such as a bunker, deep crater, or burnt-out tank may protect a sapper from mortar and artillery retaliation when the fiction supports it.

A target that cannot be seen cannot be attacked. Smoke, darkness, solid barriers, and terrain can therefore prevent an attack without increasing Defense. A successful Awareness check may reveal a concealed target until it moves or concealment changes.

## Movement

Every sapper has a **Mobility** skill. When a sapper uses their action to move, they may cross a number of squares equal to:

> 2 + Mobility, to a maximum of 12

A sapper with Mobility 0 moves 2 squares. A sapper with Mobility 3 moves 5 squares.

Use exact movement only during combat, pursuit, timed danger, or another scene where each square changes who is exposed. During ordinary exploration, move between meaningful locations on the operation map and roll only when crossing danger. Familiar safe ground may be crossed without counting squares or making checks.

Crossing mines, wire, unstable earth, open firing lanes, or other dangerous ground may require a skill check. Failure triggers retaliation as normal.

## Sapper Actions

On the sapper turn, each sapper may perform one significant action. Common actions include:

- Attack with a ranged or close-quarters weapon
- Move to a new position
- Use a tool or piece of equipment
- Attempt a battlefield task
- Help another sapper
- Begin reviving a fallen sapper
- Retreat toward safety

Simple movement or handling that does not expose the sapper to danger may be included with another action at the referee's discretion. Crossing exposed ground, climbing through wire, or moving under fire requires the sapper's action and may require a skill check.

## Attacking

To attack, build a dice pool using the relevant combat skill and weapon:

> Combat skill + weapon rating + other modifiers

Roll the pool and count successes. If the number of successes equals or exceeds the target's **Defense**, the attack inflicts a lethal hit. A target with an empty Flesh box marks it and becomes Wounded. A sapper without an empty Flesh box becomes Dying. Most ordinary enemies have no Flesh boxes and are killed immediately.

An attack normally selects one target. When a weapon, hazard, or operation explicitly allows one attack to threaten several targets, make one attack roll and compare its successes separately with each target's Defense. Apply one lethal hit to every target whose Defense is met. Effects that automatically mark Flesh or kill say so explicitly and require no attack roll.

Weapons are designed for a particular range. A weapon adds its equipment rating to the attack only when the target is within that weapon's listed range. At another range, the sapper rolls only their combat skill and other applicable modifiers. A level 2 rifle, for example, adds two dice against a target at Far range but adds no weapon dice against a target at Close range.

Defense is the combat counterpart to Resistance. It represents armor, unnatural durability, and the difficulty of landing a lethal blow. An unarmored human has Defense 1.

Armor increases Defense by its rating. Every Private receives basic armor rated 1 and therefore begins with Defense 2. Corporals and Sergeants may spend an equipment slot on higher-rated armor available to their rank. Worn armor replaces the rating of basic armor rather than adding to it.

Only armor and effects that explicitly name Defense increase it. Humanity grants Flesh boxes rather than Defense.

## Revival Under Fire

Field revival takes a sapper's full turn and leaves them exposed. Choose one fallen sapper in the same square or an adjacent square. Enough of the body must remain to identify and rebuild the sapper. Nearby donor material is assumed unless the fiction has explicitly removed or destroyed it. Field revival requires no roll. The reviving sapper can do nothing else that turn. The enemy then receives its full turn, giving it an opportunity to attack the survivor, seize the body, or force the unit away.

If the reviving sapper remains beside the body through the enemy turn, the fallen sapper returns at the beginning of the next sapper turn. If the reviver is killed or forced away, the revival fails and must be started again. Each fallen sapper must be revived separately.

A field-revived sapper returns with every Flesh box cleared and may act normally on the turn in which they return. Apply Humanity loss and every threshold crossed immediately. Any Flesh box gained by crossing a threshold begins empty. Roll and record the repair and scar immediately, but assign all newly gained skill ranks during the next between-runs step. Equipment still present on the body remains with its bearer.

The detailed timing and requirements for completing a field revival are covered in [[Death and Rebirth]].

## Enemy Actions and Formations

Ordinary enemy soldiers act in **formations** rather than taking separate turns. A formation contains two to six similar soldiers in the same area. It moves together and performs one action on the enemy turn. Named enemies, Sergeants, Battleborn, monsters, and soldiers separated from their formation act individually.

When a formation attacks, use the lead soldier's listed attack pool and add 1 die for every additional soldier contributing, to a maximum of +3 dice. The formation attacks one target unless a weapon or rule explicitly threatens several. Every killed or separated soldier immediately reduces the formation's possible bonus.

Enemy attacks follow the same rule as sapper attacks. If the enemy meets or exceeds Defense, it inflicts a lethal hit. The sapper marks an empty Flesh box and becomes Wounded, or becomes Dying if no empty box remains.

Formations keep ordinary firefights fast while making groups more dangerous than individuals. Split a formation only when its members take genuinely different positions or objectives.

Enemies should act according to their training, fear, and objectives. They may concentrate fire, retreat to fortified positions, attempt to capture bodies, or call for artillery rather than merely attacking the nearest sapper.

## Hesitation

The battlefield does not pause while the players debate. When discussion begins to drag, the referee describes an approaching threat and asks the unit for an immediate decision. If the players still hesitate, the threat occurs as retaliation without requiring a failed check.

This pressure should force decisions, not punish reasonable questions or prevent players from understanding the scene. Make the danger clear before it strikes.

## Retreat and Pursuit

Retreat is an action when exact movement matters. Combat ends when every surviving sapper reaches a known route, shelter, map edge, or other position from which the enemy cannot immediately attack them. If the enemy can pursue, continue exact movement or let the withdrawing unit attempt one appropriate cooperative check against a Resistance set by the pursuers and terrain. Success ends the pursuit. Failure changes position or costs something and the enemy acts on its normal turn; it does not create an extra combat retaliation unless a specific danger says otherwise.
